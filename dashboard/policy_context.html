GHG Protocol - The global standard for corporate emissions accounting. The Scope 2 revision (first since 2015) proposes hourly temporal matching and deliverability requirements. If adopted, it redefines every corporate renewable energy claim worldwide.
SBTi Net-Zero V2.0 - Phases in hourly matching: 50% by 2030 for large consumers (≥10 GWh/region), 90% by 2040, with 100% low-carbon electricity. Additionality tightens from 10-year to 5-year facility age by 2035. See clean firm investment case for cost implications.
RE100 - Requires 100% renewable electricity by a target year (most members: 2030–2050). Annual matching, same-market boundary. 430+ member companies representing >400 TWh annual demand.
UN 24/7 CFE Compact - Voluntary commitment to 24/7 carbon-free energy, hourly granularity, same-grid deliverability. 170+ signatories including Google, Microsoft, governments, and utilities. Preference for new-build resources.
EU CBAM - Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism. From January 2026, hourly matching required for electricity imports claiming zero-carbon status. Direct line or PPA with temporal correlation. First binding regulatory requirement for hourly matching.
EnergyTag - Develops the technical standard for Granular Energy Certificates (GECs). Now accrediting certificate issuers across 6 markets. Provides the market infrastructure layer that makes hourly matching operationally feasible at scale.

2. GHG Protocol Scope 2 Revision

Timeline

2015Current Scope 2 Guidance published
2023GHG Protocol announces Scope 2 revision
2024Survey + stakeholder workshops; draft proposals circulated
2025–2026Public consultation on proposed changes
2027Final revised standard expected

Proposed Changes

  • Hourly temporal matching - energy attribute certificates must correspond to the hour of consumption, not just the calendar year
  • Deliverability requirements - certificates must come from resources on the same grid or balancing authority, not just the same country
  • Possible exemptions - small consumers, regions without hourly tracking infrastructure, and transition periods under discussion
  • Market-based vs. location-based - both methods retained, but market-based method tightened significantly

The Competing Camps

Hourly matching (Google, Microsoft, EnergyTag): Tie procurement to the hour of consumption. Maximizes grid-cleaning incentive by ensuring demand directly stimulates dispatchable clean supply. Higher cost, higher impact.

Emissions-first (Amazon, Meta, Emissions First Partnership): Prioritize maximum tonnes CO₂ avoided per dollar spent, even if clean energy is procured in a different hour or region. Argues hourly matching over-constrains procurement and reduces climate impact.

Annual matching (CRS, legacy utility programs): Maintain annual certificate retirement. Simple, cheap, and well-understood - but increasingly disconnected from the physical grid reality that determines actual emissions impact.

3. US State Clean Energy Standards

29 states plus DC have renewable portfolio standards (RPS) or clean energy standards (CES), covering approximately 63% of US retail electricity sales. No state currently requires hourly matching - all use annual compliance. These mandates consume significant pipeline capacity, constraining what's available for voluntary corporate procurement.
