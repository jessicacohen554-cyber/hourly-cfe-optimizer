Technology Tipping Points

At what cost thresholds does the optimal clean energy mix undergo phase transitions? Small changes in technology costs can flip the dominant resource from wind-heavy to solar+storage or from renewables to clean firm power.

Resource Mix Landscape

Each bar represents a distinct optimal resource mix that wins under at least one cost scenario. The width of cost scenarios where each mix is cheapest reveals how robust or fragile each portfolio is.

Phase Transitions

When a single cost toggle (e.g., Renewable LCOE moving from Medium to High) causes the optimal portfolio's dominant resource to flip, that's a tipping point. These transitions reveal where the competitive boundary between technologies is sharpest.

Cross-Threshold Tipping Density

Higher thresholds tend to have more tipping points because the "last mile" of clean energy matching creates fierce competition between clean firm, storage, and overbuilt renewables.

Why This Matters

For investors: Tipping points reveal where your technology is one cost reduction away from dominating a market. If solar LCOE drops 10% and that flips PJM's 90% portfolio from clean-firm-dominant to solar+storage-dominant, that's a signal to invest now.

For policy makers: A subsidy or tax credit that pushes a technology past its tipping point has disproportionate impact. These maps show exactly which policy nudges create cascading market shifts.

For utilities: Portfolio robustness matters. A mix that's optimal across many cost scenarios is safer than one that's cheapest only under narrow assumptions. The mix diversity count indicates planning flexibility.
