Synthesis

Two key takeaways from the nuclear revenue crossover analysis.

The Viability Cliff

As clean energy penetration rises, wholesale prices compress and zero-price hours multiply. In most ISOs, total market revenue (energy + capacity) drops below the $41/MWh nuclear sustaining cost between 60–80% clean. This creates a cliff where nuclear goes from self-sustaining to subsidy-dependent.

The Replacement Paradox

Replacing nuclear is 2–3x more expensive than sustaining it. At 90% clean, Track 3 replacement costs typically reach $60–80/MWh — versus $41/MWh to keep nuclear running. The all new-build path (Track 2) is even costlier. The cheapest path to deep decarbonization runs through existing nuclear, not around it.

Sources & Methodology

Nuclear viability threshold ($38–44/MWh) based on all-in sustaining costs including fuel, O&M, capital, and 10% margin (NEI, Lazard LCOE 2024). LMP projections from synthetic merit-order dispatch model calibrated to 2024 ISO data. Capacity market revenue from ISO-specific capacity market constructs (PJM RPM, ISO-NE FCM, NYISO ICAP). Track cost optimization from 8,760-hour hourly matching model across 7 US ISOs.
