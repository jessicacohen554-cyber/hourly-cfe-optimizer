Why the Optimizer Rejects Short-Duration Storage

The binding constraint for hourly CFE matching isn't daily solar cycling — it's multi-day weather events. Wind droughts and extended cloud cover create gaps that 4-hour or even 8-hour batteries cannot bridge.

The daily cycle isn't the bottleneck

4-hour Li-ion batteries excel at daily arbitrage: charge during solar peak, discharge in the evening. But hourly CFE matching requires covering every hour, including multi-day low-wind/low-solar events. The optimizer consistently finds that overbuilding wind or adding clean firm capacity is cheaper than deploying batteries that can only discharge for 4–8 hours. Even at Low battery costs across all 5,832+ scenarios per region, the math never favors them.

Hydrogen: too expensive, too lossy

Green hydrogen (168-hour duration, ~40% round-trip efficiency) never appears in any optimal mix. The energy losses from electrolysis and reconversion make it uncompetitive against either LDES (100hr, 50% RTE) or clean firm generation. Even at the lowest projected hydrogen costs, the optimizer always finds a cheaper path using existing technologies.

Three Archetypes: How Regional Resources Shape Storage Need

ISOs cluster into three distinct patterns based on their existing resource endowments and how aggressively they rely on wind, clean firm, or a balanced mix.

Wind-Dominant

CAISO · ERCOT

LDES enters at 92.5% CFE in up to 94% of scenarios. Massive wind buildout provides bulk coverage; LDES fills multi-day intermittency gaps at just 1% dispatch. These ISOs keep clean firm low (10–14 GW) because cheap wind + small LDES is more economical than building firm capacity.

Balanced / Moderate

NYISO · NEISO · SPP

LDES enters at 95% CFE with moderate scenario penetration (2–52%). These ISOs balance wind and clean firm. SPP is wind-heavy but uses more diversified mixes; NYISO and NEISO have strong existing nuclear/hydro that delays storage need. LDES stays at 1% dispatch when present.

Clean Firm Dominant

PJM · MISO

LDES enters at 99.5–99.9% CFE in under 9% of scenarios. Massive clean firm buildout (up to 120 GW) makes storage nearly irrelevant. When LDES does appear, it's at 5% dispatch in extreme wind-overbuild mixes that aren't cost-optimal under Medium assumptions.

The Storage–Firm Tradeoff: Wind + LDES vs. Clean Firm Buildout

When LDES appears, it's always in wind-heavy mixes with low clean firm. The optimizer is revealing a fundamental substitution: LDES is an alternative to clean firm, not a complement.

Wind-heavy ISOs: LDES mixes are often cheaper

In ERCOT, CAISO, NYISO, and SPP, the wind + LDES pathway has a lower average cost than the clean-firm-heavy alternative at the same CFE threshold. ERCOT's cheapest LDES mix ($20.61/MWh) beats its cheapest non-LDES mix ($30.16/MWh) by 32%. The optimizer selects LDES not because storage is cheap, but because the total system cost of wind overbuild + 1% LDES is lower than building 3–7x more clean firm capacity.

Clean-firm ISOs: storage adds cost, not value

In PJM and MISO, LDES-bearing mixes are more expensive than clean-firm alternatives. PJM's LDES mixes average $95–105/MWh vs. $52–73/MWh without LDES. These ISOs have weaker VRE profiles where massive wind buildout (174–206 GW in PJM) plus storage can't compete with nuclear/clean firm buildout. Storage only enters in extreme cost scenarios where firm generation is priced out.

Key Takeaways

Short-duration storage: never cost-optimal

Across 1.1 million scenario evaluations, 4-hour batteries, 8-hour batteries, and hydrogen never appear in any cost-optimal mix. The daily charge/discharge cycle isn't the binding constraint for hourly CFE matching — multi-day weather variability is.

LDES: a last-mile gap-filler, not a foundation

100-hour iron-air LDES is the only storage technology that enters optimal mixes, and only at 92.5%+ CFE thresholds. Even then, it never exceeds 5% of demand. The optimizer achieves 90%+ CFE across all ISOs with zero storage.

LDES substitutes for clean firm, not for VRE

Where LDES appears, it's always paired with high wind / low clean firm. It enables wind-heavy ISOs (ERCOT, CAISO, SPP) to avoid expensive clean firm buildout. The total system cost of "wind + 1% LDES" often beats "3x clean firm."

Regional resources drive the storage decision

Wind-rich ISOs get LDES at 92.5–95% CFE. Nuclear-rich ISOs don't need storage until 99.5–99.9%. The divergence isn't about storage economics — it's about what alternatives each region has available.
