Storage in the Optimal Mix: ERCOT, MISO & PJM

A common assumption in clean energy planning is that massive battery and long-duration storage deployment is essential for decarbonization. Our optimization tells a different story: storage is a last-mile resource, not a foundation. Across 5,832 cost scenarios per region, 4-hour batteries never appear in any cost-optimal mix. LDES (100-hour iron-air) enters only at extreme CFE thresholds — and even then, clean firm generation does the heavy lifting.

The Headline Finding: Battery Storage Is Never Cost-Optimal

Zero battery dispatch across all three ISOs

Across ERCOT, MISO, and PJM — spanning 21 CFE thresholds (10% to 99.99%) and 5,832 cost sensitivity scenarios each — 4-hour Li-ion battery storage never appears in any cost-optimal resource mix. The optimizer consistently finds that the daily charge/discharge cycle batteries provide is not the binding constraint. The hard hours to match are multi-day weather events (wind droughts, extended cloud cover) that require either 100+ hour duration storage or dispatchable clean firm generation.

Why ERCOT gets LDES first

ERCOT's wind resource is strong but variable over multi-day timescales. The optimizer holds clean firm constant at 10 GW and solar at 15 GW from 87.5% onward, relying on wind buildout (75 → 95 GW) plus a small LDES buffer (1% dispatch) to close the gap. LDES appears in 86–94% of cost scenarios above 92.5%, but always at just 1% dispatch — it's a smoothing resource, not a backbone. ERCOT achieves 100% hourly match by 99.5% with this formula, at a remarkably low $35.66/MWh. The optimizer never needs to add clean firm capacity beyond the base 10 GW because wind + LDES is cheaper.

The clean firm ramp: 14 GW → 120 GW

The most striking feature of MISO's optimization is the dramatic pivot at 95% CFE. Below that threshold, the mix is wind-heavy (73–80 GW) with modest clean firm (14–16 GW). Above 95%, clean firm explodes while VRE shrinks: by 99.99%, clean firm is 120 GW while wind drops to 20 GW and solar to 4 GW. LDES only appears at 99.9%+ in <9% of cost scenarios (at 5% dispatch), and never in the Medium scenario. The optimizer concludes that for MISO, building more clean firm is cheaper than storing intermittent generation — likely because MISO's VRE profiles have multi-day correlation gaps that even 100-hour storage can't economically bridge.

PJM starts with 34 GW of clean firm (largely existing nuclear) and ramps to 120 GW at 99.99% CFE. The optimizer never selects storage in the Medium scenario. LDES appears in only 0.5% of cost scenarios at 99.5%, rising to 6% at 99.99% — always at 5% dispatch, always in mixes with extreme wind buildout (174–206 GW) that aren't cost-optimal under Medium assumptions. PJM's VRE mix shifts from solar-heavy at low thresholds (39 GW solar, 25 GW wind) to nearly VRE-free at high thresholds (4 GW solar, 4 GW wind at 99.5%), confirming that PJM solves hourly matching through firm capacity, not intermittent generation plus storage.

Cross-ISO Comparison: Three Paths, One Lesson

Storage is a last-mile resource

Across all three ISOs, the optimizer achieves 90%+ hourly CFE matching with zero storage. Even ERCOT — where LDES enters earliest at 92.5% — only uses it at 1% dispatch. For MISO and PJM, the optimizer reaches 99%+ CFE before storage appears in even a small fraction of cost scenarios.

4-hour batteries: never selected

Despite being the most mature and widely deployed grid storage technology, 4-hour Li-ion batteries are never part of any cost-optimal mix for these three ISOs. The binding constraint is multi-day weather variability, not daily solar cycling. The daily charge/discharge cycle batteries excel at simply isn't the bottleneck.

Clean firm is the workhorse

MISO and PJM both ramp clean firm to 120 GW at 99.99% CFE, with VRE shrinking to a small complement. ERCOT's wind resource lets it keep clean firm at 10 GW, but that's the exception driven by ERCOT's extraordinary wind capacity factors. The lesson: dispatchable clean generation is far more cost-effective than storage for achieving high hourly matching.

Regional resources drive strategy

ERCOT leans on wind + small LDES. MISO and PJM lean on clean firm. The divergence reflects fundamentally different resource endowments — ERCOT has world-class wind with high capacity factors, while MISO and PJM have weaker VRE profiles that make firm generation the more economical path to hourly matching.
