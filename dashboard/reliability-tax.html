The Reliability Tax

Excluding firm clean energy from the portfolio doesn’t avoid gas—it guarantees more gas, higher cost, and worse stranding risk.

Solar and wind are cheap and zero-carbon, but they can’t dispatch on demand. A portfolio that excludes firm clean resources—advanced nuclear, enhanced geothermal, carbon capture—can still chase 99.9% hourly carbon-free energy (CFE) matching. It just needs a massive overbuild of variable renewables and batteries to cover the gaps, plus gas peakers standing by for the hours storage can’t reach.

That gas gets built to maintain reliability, drives up system cost, and then gets stranded as clean generation scales. We modeled four technology pathways across seven US Independent System Operators (ISOs) to measure the size of that bill.

How cost accounting affects the comparison

Charts default to incremental clean cost—the full cost of building clean resources plus gas firming. A second method credits fuel savings when clean energy displaces gas generation. This shrinks costs in gas-heavy grids but inflates them in small, import-dependent grids where there’s less gas to displace.

The Bottom Line

Across most regions, including firm clean resources lowers total system cost, reduces peak gas buildout, and compresses the range of outcomes. The reliability tax is real—and it’s avoidable.
