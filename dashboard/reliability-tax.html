The Reliability Tax

What you pay when reliability is treated as a residual, not a design objective.

Grids can be clean, affordable, and reliable — but only if all three are designed for. When planning optimizes for clean and affordable and assumes reliability will just happen, reliability is paid for implicitly through a redundant fossil fleet that has to stay online to cover the hours solar, wind, and storage can’t economically serve. This analysis puts a number on that hidden cost across seven US ISOs and five 2050 CFE endpoints.

The reliability tax = the fixed costs of the gas fleet that must remain online to fill hours VRE and storage cannot economically serve, net of capacity market revenues, expressed as $/MWh of total consumption.

The Wall

Every ISO hits a cost wall somewhere between 90% and 99.9% CFE. The shape of that wall differs — and tells you how expensive each grid’s last mile is when you don’t plan for firm clean.

The hockey stick: marginal cost of the last mile
Marginal $ per CFE percentage point, Pathway 1, 2025–2050 undiscounted.
Each point is the undiscounted marginal cost of the next CFE percentage point under Pathway 1 (VRE + batteries + existing nuclear). Values near zero indicate a plateau — additional percentage points are physically unreachable. Negative values mean moving to the higher endpoint is actually cheaper because the optimizer restructures the mix.
Across seven ISOs
Every grid hits a wall — but the wall looks different in every region.
Under Pathway 1, seven ISOs reach 95% CFE at widely different costs, and only a subset can climb further. Solar-heavy CAISO and wind-heavy ERCOT/MISO hit their walls early; hydro- and offshore-wind-backed NEISO and NYISO coast further on existing clean capacity. The shape of each curve is the signature of the underlying resource mix.
