The Reliability Tax

The electric grid balanced two dimensions for a century: reliability and affordability. Deep decarbonization demands a third—clean. Ignoring reliability while chasing the other two comes with a bill.

The electric grid became what it is today by balancing two dimensions: reliability and affordability. It delivered energy precisely when and where people needed it. Deep decarbonization introduces a third dimension—clean—and the temptation is to optimize for affordability and clean while treating reliability as an afterthought.

Variable renewable energy (VRE) scores perfectly on two axes. Solar and wind are cheap and zero-carbon. But a grid that chases 99.9% carbon-free energy (CFE) matching with VRE and storage alone must overbuild massively and lean on gas peakers to fill the gaps. That gas gets built, then stranded. That’s the reliability tax.

This analysis compares four technology pathways across seven US Independent System Operators (ISOs) to quantify what reliability costs when it’s deferred versus when it’s designed in from the start.

Cost mode sensitivity: incremental clean cost vs net-of-gas-savings

All charts on this page default to incremental clean cost (the full cost of building clean resources plus gas firming). An alternative accounting method credits gas fuel savings when clean energy displaces fossil generation. This reduces costs in gas-heavy grids but increases them in small, import-dependent grids where gas displacement is limited.
