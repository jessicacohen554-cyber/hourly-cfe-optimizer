Step 5B (SBTi Net-Zero) vs Step 10 (Market Reference Case)

Do SBTi net-zero pathway prices converge with market-driven reference case outcomes?

Same Grid, Different Questions

Step 5B — Policy-Driven Snapshot

Question: "Given we ARE at X% clean, what do wholesale prices look like?"

  • Physics-optimized resource mix (efficient frontier)
  • Static 2025 snapshot — no deployment trajectory
  • Merit-order fossil stack with heat rate curves
  • Demand-quantile adjustments for scarcity pricing

Step 10 — Market-Driven Trajectory

Question: "What clean% does the market reach profitably?"

  • Profit-maximizing resource deployment
  • 2023–2050 trajectory with learning curves
  • Same LMP engine (merit-order fossil stack)
  • Revenue stacking: energy + capacity + RECs
Both models use the same LMP engine — merit-order fossil dispatch with heat rate curves, fuel price sensitivity, and demand-quantile scarcity adjustments. Differences arise from which resource mix is deployed at each clean energy percentage, not from the price formation mechanism itself.

Why the Gap Is Large

These models measure fundamentally different things and the delta is expected:

  • Step 5B avg_lmp = the system-average wholesale price after the clean mix is established. At 70% clean, fossil units are displaced and the remaining marginal units set lower clearing prices.
  • Step 10 avg_lmp = the wholesale price at the time of deployment, reflecting the fossil stack that existed before the zone was built. This is the price signal that drove the investment decision — not the price after the new capacity enters.

Step 10 shows what prices were when generators decided to deploy; Step 5B shows what prices become once they're running. The gap is the classic "merit-order effect" — new clean generation suppresses the very prices that justified its deployment.

This is not a model error. The gap quantifies the merit-order effect of clean energy deployment. A large gap (Step 10 >> Step 5B) means new clean resources significantly suppressed wholesale prices — exactly the dynamic that drives the "missing money problem" in restructured electricity markets.

Verdict

Where They Converge

  • Same clean% + same mix → prices converge (identical LMP engine)
  • Below ~65% clean → both models produce similar wholesale prices because fossil stack dominates price formation regardless of clean mix composition
  • Energy-only revenue tracks closely at moderate clean penetration

Where They Diverge

  • Same clean% + different mix → prices diverge due to resource composition differences
  • Above ~75% clean → structural divergence (policy vs market incentives)
  • Step 10 deploys profit-maximizing mix (more solar, less nuclear); Step 5B uses physics-optimized EF mix
  • Step 10 includes REC revenue that shifts deployment economics
This is not an apples-to-apples comparison — and that's by design. Both models are valid for their respective purposes. Convergence below 65% validates the shared LMP engine. Divergence above 75% reflects the genuine difference between "what physics requires" and "what markets deliver."
