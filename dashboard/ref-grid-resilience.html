Grid Resilience Stress Testing

How robust are optimized clean energy portfolios under worst-case weather? Synthetic stress tests — heat waves, polar vortices, wind droughts, and compound events — reveal which regions and thresholds are most vulnerable.

Resilience Heatmap

Each cell shows the resilience ratio: stressed CFE score divided by the baseline score. Values above 0.95 (green) indicate a robust portfolio; below 0.85 (red) signals significant vulnerability to that stress scenario.

Scenario Details

Detailed metrics for each stress scenario at the selected threshold. Max gap duration measures the longest consecutive streak of hours where clean supply falls below 50% of demand.

CFE Degradation Across Thresholds

How does each stress scenario degrade portfolio performance at different CFE targets? Counter-intuitively, higher-threshold portfolios often maintain higher resilience ratios because their storage buffers absorb weather shocks.

Maximum Gap Duration

The worst-case consecutive hours where clean supply fails to meet 50% of demand. Shorter gaps indicate faster recovery from stress events.

Why This Matters

For grid planners: A portfolio that scores 95% under average weather but collapses to 70% during a polar vortex isn't actually a 95% portfolio — it's a fair-weather portfolio. Stress testing reveals the true performance floor.

For corporate buyers: If your 24/7 CFE commitment is undermined by a single heat wave, the credibility cost is far higher than the incremental cost of a more resilient portfolio. Storage and firm power are insurance premiums, not waste.

Key insight: Portfolios with storage (battery + LDES) consistently show higher resilience ratios than pure-renewable portfolios at the same threshold. The storage buffer absorbs short-duration weather shocks, maintaining CFE scores even when generation profiles degrade.
