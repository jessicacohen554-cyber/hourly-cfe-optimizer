LMP Cannibalization: The Standalone VRE Trap

5

The Three-Headed Challenge

Price Cannibalization

Zero-marginal-cost generation drives wholesale prices toward $0 during peak production. CAISO solar captures <30% of average LMP; ERCOT wind at 54%.

Basis Risk

Nodal price divergence from congestion means power generated in cheap zones can’t reach load. Hyperscalers now discount PPAs for basis exposure.

ELCC Collapse

PJM assigns solar ELCC of ~8%. A 100 MW solar farm gets paid for 8 MW of capacity. Standalone VRE capacity value is effectively zero.

Bottom line: A merchant standalone solar asset modeling standard forward curves without adjusting for capture-rate degradation will fail its DSCR and equity IRR hurdles. The standalone VRE investment thesis is structurally broken.

The 24/7 CFE Premium: Why Hyperscalers Will Pay More

6

From Annual Matching to Hourly CFE

The shift from annual volumetric matching to 24/7 hourly CFE is creating a structural pricing premium that fundamentally changes VRE economics.

Annual matching: Buy RECs to offset total consumption. Allows fossil reliance at night. Low cost, low impact.
24/7 hourly CFE: Match consumption with clean generation every hour. Requires storage to cover night/low-wind. Higher cost, real emissions reduction.

Validated by Landmark Deals

  • Microsoft + Constellation: 835 MW Three Mile Island nuclear restart — 20-year firm PPA at premium pricing
  • Google + Broadwing: 400 MW gas + CCS — paying for both power AND carbon capture
  • Google + AES: 24/7 CFE portfolios using solar + wind + storage arrays
  • Meta + Constellation: 20-year Clinton nuclear PPA for firm, 24/7 clean baseload
  • Amazon + Talen: 1.92 GW Susquehanna nuclear — largest single-asset nuclear PPA
Product Availability Paradox (Eurelectric/EY): Extreme complexity of structuring 24/7 CFE products creates supply scarcity → higher margins for developers who can deliver.

Procurement Accounting: What It Means for Where to Buy

11

Three competing procurement strategies are fragmenting the market — but the VRE+Storage investment thesis holds under all of them. Strategy choice shapes where to weight the portfolio, not whether to invest.

Three Strategies, One Thesis

① Consequential (Cross-Regional)
Buy cheapest $/tCO₂ clean energy anywhere in the US. No hourly constraint. Buyer picks lowest-cost ISO. Drives new-build in MISO/SPP/ERCOT where wind is cheapest.
Buyers: Meta, Amazon (large MISO/SPP unbundled REC purchases). Incentive: Cheapest path to high CFE % — VRE pipeline in wind-rich ISOs is the product.
② Hourly Matching (Same-ISO)
Match consumption with clean generation every hour, same ISO. Forces storage + firm pairing. Requires 1.7× over-procurement at 95% CFE to cover nights and low-wind.
Buyers: Google (24/7 CFE), Microsoft (nuclear PPAs). Incentive: VRE+Storage is the only product that satisfies hourly matching — mandatory pairing = higher $/MWh but guaranteed offtake.
③ Annual Volumetric (Status Quo)
Match total MWh over the year. Cheapest unbundled RECs satisfy targets — high paper CFE %, low emissions impact. Being phased out by leading buyers.
Incentive: Minimal VRE pipeline value — existing RECs suffice. Sunset strategy as SBTi/SEC tighten standards.

Analytical Methodology

Demand Forecasting (Slide 2)

Multi-source triangulation of DC power demand from BNEF, Goldman Sachs, IEA, and Wood Mackenzie. Pipeline figures from DC Byte site-level tracking (2,073 US sites). GW-to-TWh conversion uses weighted capacity factors by technology (solar 25%, wind 35%, nuclear 90%).

Gap Analysis (Slide 4)

Bottom-up aggregation of hyperscaler clean energy deals from public filings, press releases, and sustainability reports. De-duplicated to remove framework-level entries. Gap = 2030 projected demand minus contracted supply at company level.

Capture Rate Projections (Slide 5)

Historical capture rates from S&P Global Market Intelligence (2019–2025 actuals). Forward projections modeled as exponential decay tied to VRE penetration share, calibrated to CAISO solar (<30% at 25% penetration) and ERCOT wind (54% at 30% penetration). Validated against Lazard LCOE+ v18.0 and LevelTen PPA price indices.

Storage Economics (Slide 7)

Cost data from Lazard LCOS v9.0 and BNEF Battery Price Survey. Dispatch economics from Constellation Hourly CFE Optimizer (8,760-hour merit-order dispatch with storage charge/discharge optimization). Arbitrage revenue benchmarked against Modo Energy ERCOT BESS actual performance data.

Wright’s Law (Slide 8)

Learning rates from cumulative deployment data: Solar PV ~24% (Swanson’s Law, 1976–2024), Li-ion ~18% (BNEF, 2010–2025), Wind ~15% (IRENA, 2000–2024). SMR and EGS rates estimated from ITIF (2025) and Princeton/Ricks-Jenkins (2025). Formula: C(x) = C₀ × x^(ln(1-LR)/ln(2)).
