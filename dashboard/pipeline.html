Pipeline Architecture

The 8,760 Problem Optimizer — data pipeline, analysis tracks, and methodology

About the Model

The 8,760 Problem Optimizer

A procurement-focused hourly CFE optimizer that co-optimizes resource mix and cost across 9 sensitivity dimensions. Full 8,760-hour resolution, 7 U.S. ISOs, >5,000 cost scenarios per region. The model takes the grid as-given and answers: what portfolio of clean energy should a buyer procure to meet hourly matching targets at minimum cost? Not a system planning model — a procurement tool built on observed EIA data anchored to a 2025 snapshot.

Pipeline Architecture (Steps 0–7)

Steps 0–4 run sequentially. Step 1 is compute-intensive (hours); Steps 2–4 are cheap (seconds to minutes). Step 5 builds the dispatch cache, then cache-independent scripts run in parallel. Step 6 scripts depend on the dispatch cache and run in parallel. Step 7 aggregates all results into the dashboard.

Analysis Tracks

Three analysis tracks share the same Physics Feasible Space but apply different resource constraints and cost assumptions. All tracks read from data/step2-ef-parquets/.

Track 1 — ECF

Existing Clean Floor (Baseline)

Cost optimization built on 2025 absolute existing clean generation. Existing resources priced at $0 (sunk fleet). New-build procurement optimized on top.

  • All resources available: solar, wind, nuclear, CCS, hydro, battery, LDES
  • Hydro capped at existing regional capacity
  • Nuclear uprates included (cheapest clean firm tranche)
  • Existing clean TWh constant; share declines with demand growth
  • 5,832 cost scenarios per ISO/threshold (17,496 for CAISO)
  • LMP module runs on this track
  • Script: scripts/step3_cost_optimization.py
Track 2 — NB

New-Build / Greenfield

What hourly matching incentivizes to build from scratch. Ignores ALL existing clean energy — no existing solar, wind, nuclear, CCS, or hydro credited.

  • Hydro excluded (hydro=0 mixes only)
  • Nuclear uprates ON (cheapest new-build option, part of greenfield procurement)
  • All other resources at new-build LCOE pricing
  • Shows the “pure market signal” — what hourly mandates would build
  • Script: scripts/step3_track_nb_ctr.py --track nb
Track 3 — CTR

Cost to Replace

What it costs to replace existing nuclear/hydro with alternatives. Uprates disabled — only new-build nuclear at full LCOE. Hydro at existing capacity.

  • Nuclear uprates OFF — only new-build nuclear at full LCOE
  • Hydro at existing regional capacity (existing-only)
  • Answers “what’s the cost if we lose this plant?”
  • Shows asset stranding risk and replacement premium
  • Script: scripts/step3_track_nb_ctr.py --track ctr

How It Compares

The optimizer sits in a distinct niche — more granular than system planning models, more rigorous than LCOE screens.

vs. Capacity Expansion Models

GenX, ReEDS, SWITCH, PLEXOS

Capacity expansion models optimize the entire power system — generation, transmission, retirements, and entry — over multi-decade horizons. They answer “what should the grid build?” We answer “what should a buyer procure today?” Our model takes the existing grid as fixed and optimizes procurement portfolios within it. We share the same hourly resolution as GenX (8,760 hours) but focus on cost sensitivity and portfolio co-optimization rather than system planning.

vs. Economy-Wide / IAMs

GCAM, TIMES, REMIND, E3ME

Integrated assessment models simulate entire economies — energy, land use, industry, transport — over century timescales. They trade temporal resolution for scope: annual or 5-year timesteps vs. our hourly granularity. We don’t model cross-sector feedbacks, GDP impacts, or macro-economic equilibria. We go deep on one question — hourly clean energy procurement — with a level of temporal and cost-sensitivity detail that economy-wide models can’t support.

vs. LCOE / Screening Curves

Lazard, NREL ATB, EIA AEO levelized costs

LCOE analysis compares technologies on a $/MWh basis but ignores when power is generated. Solar at $30/MWh is meaningless if you need power at midnight. We enforce hourly matching — every MWh of demand must be met in the hour it occurs. This is why our optimal mixes include storage and firm generation that LCOE screening would discard as “too expensive.”

vs. Dispatch / Production Cost Models

PLEXOS (dispatch), GE MAPS, PROMOD, Aurora

Production cost models simulate detailed unit commitment — minimum generation constraints, ramp rates, start-up costs, ancillary services. We use simplified dispatch (capacity-constrained, no unit commitment) because our objective is portfolio cost optimization across thousands of sensitivity scenarios, not operational simulation of a single grid state. We trade dispatch fidelity for breadth of cost exploration.

What It Can & Can’t Do

Strengths

  • Full 8,760-hour matching — not reduced time-slices or representative weeks. Captures seasonal nuclear derates, summer solar peaks, winter wind patterns
  • Cost + mix co-optimization — re-optimizes the resource portfolio for every cost scenario. Reveals when cost assumptions change the optimal strategy, not just the price
  • 9-dimensional parametric sensitivity with >5,000 scenarios per region. Shows the full uncertainty landscape, not a single “base case”
  • Merit-order clean firm tranche pricing — uprates fill first (cheapest), then geothermal (CAISO), then new nuclear or CCS (whichever cheaper). Reflects real supply curves
  • Two-tier pricing — existing capacity at $0 (sunk fleet), new-build at LCOE + transmission. Captures the procurement reality that existing is free and new is expensive
  • Storage dispatch with physical constraints — battery (4hr daily cycle, 85% RTE) and LDES (100hr multi-day, 50% RTE) both capacity-limited. No unrealistic perfect-foresight dispatch
  • CO₂ dispatch attribution with merit-order retirement — coal retires first, then oil, then gas. GHG Protocol Scope 2 compliant
  • Policy analysis — 45Q tax credit toggle, demand growth sweep, NB/CTR track comparison. Answers “what happens if this policy changes?”
  • Regional specificity — 7 ISOs with distinct generation profiles, demand shapes, wholesale prices, and fossil fuel mixes. Not a national average

Limitations

  • No transmission expansion or congestion — uses existing interconnection costs as static adders. Doesn’t model whether transmission can physically deliver the resources
  • No inter-regional trade — each ISO is self-contained. Understates feasibility for import-dependent regions (e.g., NEISO could import Canadian hydro)
  • No unit commitment — simplified dispatch without minimum generation, ramp rates, or start-up costs. May overstate flexibility of firm resources
  • No reliability / ELCC constraints — mixes meet hourly energy matching but don’t enforce peak capacity reserve margins. High-solar mixes may technically match hours but lack capacity adequacy
  • Static 2025 snapshot — no multi-year buildout trajectory, learning curves, or retirement schedules. Can’t answer “when should I build?”
  • Fixed demand profiles — no demand response, load flexibility, or electrification shifts. Demand shapes are immutable inputs
  • No hydrogen storage — excluded as commercially immature. May understate long-duration options for extreme scenarios
  • Static LDES cost — LCOS is fixed regardless of utilization. Low-utilization scenarios may understate true cost; high-utilization may overstate it
  • CCS modeled as baseload under 45Q — assumes 100% capacity factor when 45Q is active (economic incentive to maximize capture). May overstate CCS output in shoulder hours

Appendix: Detailed Calculation Methodology

Technical reference for each pipeline step. Covers algorithms, formulas, order of operations, and physical constraints.

0 Data Fetch & Normalization

scripts/step0_*.py — 7 scripts

Data Sources

  • EIA API v2 — Hourly generation by fuel type and demand for all 7 ISOs (2021–2025). Source: EIA-930 Hourly Electric Grid Monitor.
  • EPA eGRID 2023 — Plant-level CO₂ emission rates (lb CO₂/MWh) by fuel type, mapped to parent companies and ISO regions.
  • gridstatus — 2025 hourly day-ahead LMP for 5 ISOs (CAISO, ERCOT, PJM, NYISO, NEISO).

Multi-Year Averaging & Normalization

Generation and demand profiles are averaged across 2021–2025 to smooth year-to-year weather variability, then scaled to 2025 annual totals. This preserves the hourly shape (diurnal and seasonal patterns) while anchoring absolute levels to the most recent data year.

profile_normalized[h] = avg_2021_2025[h] / sum(avg_2021_2025) * annual_total_2025

Timezone Corrections

EIA profiles arrive in UTC. Two correction scripts handle the conversion: step0_fix_utc_profiles.py converts to local prevailing time for LMP/peak-hour analysis, and step0_fix_dst_profiles.py handles DST transitions (spring forward: interpolate missing hour; fall back: average duplicate hour). Solar profiles are DST-corrected to ensure peak generation aligns with local noon.

MISO & SPP Consolidation

MISO and SPP data arrives as per-year JSON files from the EIA API. step0_consolidate_miso_spp.py merges these into the same consolidated format as the other 5 ISOs, producing unified eia_generation_profiles.json, eia_demand_profiles.json, and eia_fossil_mix.json for downstream consumption.

1 Physics Feasible Space (PFS) Generator

scripts/step1_pfs_generator.py — Most compute-intensive step (~4 hours)

4D Resource Grid Search

The optimizer searches all valid 4-dimensional resource allocations where clean_firm + solar + wind + hydro = 100% (each 0–100%, hydro capped at regional existing). The search proceeds in two phases:

  • Phase 1 (Coarse 5%): All integer combinations at 5% step resolution (~250–300 base mixes per ISO). Plus 30 predefined “archetype” seed mixes (pure solar, pure wind, balanced, CF-dominant) to ensure extreme solution spaces aren’t missed.
  • Phase 2 (Fine 1%): For each mix proven feasible in Phase 1, generate fine combos in ±2% radius at 1% resolution using vectorized meshgrid (~100–200 additional combos per feasible archetype).

Procurement Sweep

Instead of binary search per mix, the optimizer evaluates all N mixes at each procurement level simultaneously via a single numpy operation. Procurement ranges are threshold-adaptive:

ThresholdProcurement RangeStep Size
50%50–200%5–10%
80%80–250%5%
100%100–350%2–5%

Cross-threshold pruning: if mix X needed 95% procurement at threshold 80%, the search starts at 95% for threshold 85% (monotonicity — minimum feasible procurement only increases with higher thresholds).

Hourly Match Score

The score is energy-weighted (not hour-count):

score = sum(min(demand[h], supply[h]) for h in 0..8759) / sum(demand[h] for h in 0..8759) × 100

Demand is re-normalized to sum to exactly 1.0. Each hour’s contribution equals the energy matched that hour, so partially-matched hours count proportionally. A score of 90% means 90% of total annual demand-energy was met hour-by-hour.

Two-Phase Adaptive Storage Sweep

After the resource grid search identifies candidate mixes, storage levels are swept in two phases:

  • Phase 1 (Coarse 0.25%): Battery 4hr [0, 0.25, 0.5, ... 2.5%], Battery 8hr [0, 0.25, ... 3.0%], LDES [0, 0.5, 1.0, ... 10%] of demand. Total: 8 × 9 × 9 = 648 storage combos per mix.
  • Saturation Analysis: After the coarse sweep, the optimizer identifies the “saturation point” — the storage level beyond which additional storage no longer improves the score. Example: if battery 4hr saturates at 1.2%, levels >1.2% are wasted compute.
  • Phase 2 (Fine 0.05%): Within the saturation range, batteries are re-swept at 0.05% steps. Example: if bat4 saturates at 1.2%, fine sweep covers [0, 0.05, 0.10, ... 1.45%]. LDES stays coarse (less sensitive to fine granularity).

Battery Dispatch Physics (4hr, Daily Cycle)

The 4-hour lithium-ion battery operates on a 24-hour daily cycle with capacity specified as % of annual demand. Power rating = capacity / 4 hours. Round-trip efficiency = 85%.

For each day (0-364): CHARGE PHASE (sequential through 24 hours): surplus_available = max(0, supply[h] - demand[h]) charge = min(surplus_available, power_rating, remaining_capacity - stored) stored += charge DISCHARGE PHASE (sequential through 24 hours): available_energy = stored × 0.85 (RTE applied) gap = max(0, demand[h] - supply[h]) discharge = min(gap, power_rating, available_energy) dispatched += discharge available_energy -= discharge

Charges only from leftover surplus after all other generation sources. Discharge constrained by both power rate (capacity/4) and available stored energy. Post-battery residual flows to battery 8hr, then LDES.

Battery 8hr Dispatch (2-Day Window)

Same algorithm as 4hr but with 48-hour window (2 days) instead of 24, reflecting realistic ~200 cycles/year. Operates on post-battery-4hr residual surplus/gap. Power = capacity / 8 hours. RTE = 85%.

LDES Dispatch (100hr Iron-Air, 7-Day Window)

Long-duration energy storage uses a 7-day (168-hour) rolling window. Capacity specified as % of demand. Power = capacity / 100 hours. Round-trip efficiency = 50%.

For each 7-day window (52 windows/year): SOC = state of charge (carries from previous window) CHARGE PHASE: surplus from post-battery residual charge = min(surplus, power_rating, energy_capacity - SOC) SOC += charge DISCHARGE PHASE: gap from post-battery residual discharge = min(gap, power_rating, SOC × 0.50) SOC -= discharge / 0.50 (energy consumed > energy delivered)

Can accumulate multi-day surplus before discharging. On discharge, SOC -= discharge / efficiency to correctly track true energy consumed from storage (more stored energy is depleted than delivered).

2 Efficient Frontier Extraction

scripts/step2_efficient_frontier.py — ~30 seconds per ISO

Phase 1: Threshold Score Gate

Filters PFS rows to include only those matching target thresholds {50, 55, 60, 65, 70, 75, 80, 85, 87.5, 90, 92.5, 95, 97.5, 99, 100}. Typical reduction: ~21M → ~18.6M rows.

Phase 2: Global Dedup & Pareto-Optimal Procurement

The critical insight: different mixes at different thresholds may have identical resource allocations. The EF keeps only the Pareto-optimal procurement variants per unique allocation.

Allocation Uniqueness

A unique allocation is defined by 7 dimensions: clean_firm, solar, wind, hydro (int 0–100) plus battery_dispatch_pct, battery8_dispatch_pct, ldes_dispatch_pct (float, 0.05% granularity). These are packed into a single int64 key via base-encoding:

group_key = CF × (101³ × 2001³) + Sol × (101² × 2001³) + Wnd × (101 × 2001³) + Hyd × 2001³ + Bat_key × 2001² + Bat8_key × 2001 + LDES_key where storage keys = round(dispatch_pct × 20) to convert 0.05% steps to integers

Segmented Cumulative Maximum

Within each allocation group, rows are sorted by (procurement ascending, score descending). A Numba-compiled segmented cumulative maximum identifies rows where score strictly exceeds all predecessors at lower procurement. These are the Pareto-optimal points: no other row has ≤ procurement AND ≥ score.

For each allocation group (sorted by procurement asc): cummax[0] = score[0] cummax[i] = max(cummax[i-1], score[i]) Keep row if: score[i] > cummax[i-1] (strictly improves on all previous) Also keep: first row in each group (lowest procurement point)

Threshold Column Dropped

The output EF does not include a threshold column. This enables cross-threshold picking in Step 3: a mix with score=89% can satisfy a threshold=80% requirement at lower cost than a mix specifically optimized for 80%. Step 3 filters post-hoc: score ≥ threshold.

Typical reduction: 21M PFS → 18.6M after gate → ~3M after dedup → ~1.8M EF after Pareto filter (~91% total reduction).

Coefficient Matrix Construction

The optimizer pre-computes a (N_mixes × 10) coefficient matrix where each column holds a quantity coefficient. For each scenario, a 10-element price vector is constructed from LCOE tables. Total cost = coefficient matrix × price vector + constant (gas backup).

total_cost[i] = Σ(coeff[i,k] × price[k]) + constant[i] Columns: 0: Wholesale ($0 for existing = sunk fleet) 1: Solar new-build (% above existing share × LCOE + tx) 2: Wind new-build (% above existing share × LCOE + tx) 3: CCS new-build (% × LCOE + tx) 4: Nuclear uprate (TWh basis, capped) 5: Geothermal (TWh basis, CAISO only) 6: Tranche 3 remaining CF (min of nuclear/CCS price) 7: Battery 4hr (% × LCOE + tx) 8: Battery 8hr (% × LCOE + tx) 9: LDES 100hr (% × LCOE + tx)

Existing Resources = $0

Existing clean generation is priced at $0 (sunk fleet — already built and operating). Only new-build above the existing share incurs LCOE costs:

sol_demand_pct = (procurement_pct / 100) × solar_pct sol_existing_pct = min(sol_demand_pct, existing_solar_share[iso]) sol_new_pct = max(0, sol_demand_pct - sol_existing_pct) cost_solar = sol_new_pct / 100 × (LCOE_solar[level][iso] + TX_solar[level][iso])

Optimal Mix Selection

For each (scenario, threshold) pair, the optimizer selects the single mix with minimum total cost among all mixes where hourly_match_score ≥ threshold (with 100% mapped to 99.5% gate for floating-point safety).

A Numba-compiled bucketed argmin processes all 21 thresholds in O(N) instead of O(21N): a single pass assigns each mix to its highest-qualifying threshold bucket, then a cumulative min sweep propagates the cheapest mix downward from highest to lowest threshold.

NEISO Winter Gas Pipeline Constraint

New England faces pipeline bottlenecks during winter heating season (Algonquin Citygates). CCS-CCGT plants in NEISO incur an additional adder:

CCS gas adder = heat_rate(7) × winter_premium($7.50) × winter_fraction(0.25) = +$13.13/MWh (annualized) Wholesale adder = +$4.00/MWh (annualized winter gas on marginal pricing)

45Q Tax Credit Correction

When 45Q is enabled, CCS receives a $27.5/MWh offset (corrected from the original $29.0 optimizer value). The without-45Q overlay recomputes all costs with the credit disabled, enabling analysis of CCS viability without the federal subsidy.

CCS vs LDES Crossover Analysis

Computes the capacity factor at which CCS becomes cheaper than LDES after removing 45Q:

crossover_CF = (CCS_no45q × 0.63 × 0.85) / ((LDES_cost - CCS_TX) - CCS_no45q × 0.37) where: 0.37 = fraction of CCS cost that scales with CF 0.63 = complement (fixed/baseload fraction) 0.85 = CCS reference capacity factor (NETL Baseline Rev 4a)

Gas Capacity Backup & Resource Adequacy

Required_fossil_MW = (Peak_demand_MW × 1.15 - Clean_peak_MW) / GAF Clean_peak_MW = Σ(resource_% × avg_demand_MW × ELCC_credit[resource]) GAF = Gas availability factor (0.82–0.88 by ISO) ELCC credits: nuclear=1.0, solar=0.30, wind=0.10, CCS/gas=0.90, battery/LDES=0.95, hydro=0.50

5 Dispatch Cache

scripts/step5_build_dispatch_cache.py

What the Dispatch Cache Is

A pre-computed archive of full 8,760-hour dispatch results for every unique resource mix across all ISOs. Eliminates redundant dispatch reconstruction in Step 6 scripts — each mix is computed once and cached.

How It’s Created

  • Extract unique mixes: Read Step 4 parquets, deduplicate by (resource_pcts, procurement, battery, battery8, LDES) → typically 50–500 unique mixes per ISO.
  • Build supply matrix: Pre-compute a (5 × 8760) numpy array of supply profiles once per ISO (nuclear with seasonal derate, DST-corrected solar).
  • Reconstruct dispatch: For each unique mix, call reconstruct_hourly_dispatch(detailed=True) which runs the full merit-order dispatch (CF → CCS → hydro → wind → solar), then battery 4hr, battery 8hr, and LDES dispatch in sequence.
  • Store as NPZ: Each mix is keyed by an archetype string (resource percentages + dispatch parameters) and stored as arrays in a NumPy .npz file.

6 Dispatch-Dependent Analytics

6 scripts, all reading from the Step 5 dispatch cache

6a. CO₂ Recompute (step6_recompute_co2.py)

Merit-order fuel retirement: Coal retires first, then oil, then gas.

Tier 1 (clean < baseline): All coal, oil, gas in current proportions Tier 2 (50% < clean < 70%): Coal retires first (capped at COAL_CAP_TWH), then oil (OIL_CAP_TWH) Tier 3 (clean ≥ 70%): All coal and oil fully retired; only gas remains Tier 4 (clean = 100%): Baseline fleet reference Displaced emission rate = Σ(fuel_displaced_twh × rate[fuel]) / total_displaced_twh CO₂ abated = fossil_displaced_mwh × displaced_rate - CCS_supply × CCS_residual_rate

Fast path: Uses pre-computed hourly_match_score for ~1000x speedup (scalar math instead of 8760-hour reconstruction). Dispatch path: Falls back to full hourly reconstruction via dispatch cache when match score is unavailable.

6b. Compressed Day Profiles (step6_compressed_day.py)

Compresses 8,760-hour dispatch to 24-hour representative day profiles by summing each hour-of-day across all 365 days:

compressed[hour_of_day] = sum(dispatch[hour_of_day + day*24] for day in 0..364) Applied to: demand, matched_{resource}, surplus_{resource}, battery_charge, ldes_charge, gap Output keyed by: ISO / threshold / mix_key (e.g., "20_30_15_0_5_100_50_10")

6c. MAC Statistics (step6_compute_mac_stats.py)

6 MAC (Marginal Abatement Cost) metrics computed per ISO:

  • Fan chart (P10/P25/P50/P75/P90): Percentiles of MAC = effective_cost × demand_mwh / co2_abated across all scenarios per threshold.
  • Stepwise marginal MAC: Δcost / ΔCO₂ between adjacent thresholds. Only steps where ΔCO₂ > 0.
  • Monotonic envelope: Running max of raw MAC (enforces non-decreasing cost curve).
  • Path-constrained MAC: Medium scenario only; enforces monotonically non-decreasing resource deployment (no capacity retirements between thresholds).
  • ANOVA sensitivity decomposition: Per-toggle eta-squared = SS_between / SS_total to identify which cost toggle explains the most MAC variance.
  • Benchmark crossover: Threshold where MAC crosses EPA SCC ($190/ton), EU ETS ($88/ton), DAC ($400–600/ton).

6d. LMP Pricing (step6_compute_lmp_prices.py)

Three-layer synthetic LMP model calibrated to PJM SOM 2024:

  • Layer 1 — Merit-order dispatch: Cost-based offer formula (PJM Manual 15): MC = (HR × fuel + VOM + CO₂ × CO₂_price) × 1.10. Heat rates: coal 10.0, CCGT 7.0, CT 10.5 MMBtu/MWh. Stack walk via np.searchsorted.
  • Layer 2 — Load-dependent heat rate ramp: 15% quadratic variation within each merit-order band simulates partial-load efficiency loss.
  • Layer 3 — Demand-quantile pricing: High-demand (>80th pct) congestion adder up to $60/MWh. Scarcity tail (>97th pct) exponential up to $500/MWh. Low-demand (<15th pct) negative pricing floor at -$25/MWh.

6e. Consequential Queue (step6_consequential_queue.py)

Cross-regional deployment path that minimizes marginal $/tCO₂ at each step. Zones: 50→75% (cheap renewables), 75→90% (firm power needed), 90→99% (last-mile premium). Uses dispatch cache for hourly emission accounting via compute_co2_from_dispatch().

6f. Scenario Comparison (step6_scenario_comparison.py)

Dual-scenario comparison: Scenario A (pure consequential — chase cheapest $/tCO₂ sequentially) vs Scenario B (hourly matching — co-optimize full mix at target threshold). Both use dispatch-cache-based emission accounting to ensure consistent CO₂ methodology.
