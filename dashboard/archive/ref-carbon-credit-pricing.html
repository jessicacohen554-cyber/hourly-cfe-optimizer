What Should a Carbon Credit Actually Cost?

Using physics-grounded marginal abatement costs from hourly dispatch modeling to derive fair-value carbon credit prices. A credit backed by hourly-matched clean energy in PJM is fundamentally different from a generic offset — and should be priced accordingly.

Carbon Market Benchmarks

Current carbon pricing spans three orders of magnitude, from $5 voluntary credits to $600 DAC removal. This model's MAC curves provide a physics-based anchor between these extremes.

Marginal Abatement Cost as Credit Value

The MAC at each CFE threshold represents the true cost of displacing one additional ton of CO₂ through hourly-matched clean energy procurement. This IS the fair-value carbon credit price — the cost a buyer would need to pay for verifiable, physics-verified emission displacement.

The Hourly Matching Quality Premium

Hourly-matched clean energy credits are more rigorous than annual matching. The quality premium quantifies the additional cost of temporal precision — and the additional environmental integrity it represents.

Most voluntary carbon credits are massively underpriced. A $12/tCO₂ voluntary credit (Verra average) is 5-15x below the actual cost of displacing grid emissions through hourly clean energy matching. This gap represents either greenwashing or a genuinely different (lower quality) approach to emission reduction.

Implications

For corporate buyers: If you're buying voluntary carbon credits at $10-15/tCO₂, you're buying paper, not physics. The actual cost of hourly-matched emission displacement is $75-200+/tCO₂ in most regions. The gap between market price and physical cost is the "greenwashing discount."

For credit markets: These MAC curves provide a fair-value floor for high-integrity credits. Credits backed by verified hourly matching should command a premium over annual or unbundled EACs — this analysis quantifies that premium.

Important caveat: The quality premium estimates here use a simplified 60% ratio for annual matching MAC. Actual annual matching costs vary by region and depend on the availability of baseload clean resources. The directional insight (hourly > annual cost) is robust; the exact premium will vary.

For policy makers: Carbon border adjustments and compliance market reforms should account for matching rigor. An EU ETS credit at $88/tCO₂ may not be comparable to a US voluntary credit at $12/tCO₂ — but a US hourly-matched credit at $120/tCO₂ may actually exceed EU compliance quality.
