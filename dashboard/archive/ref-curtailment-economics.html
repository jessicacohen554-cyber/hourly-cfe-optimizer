Reference / Curtailment Economics

The Hidden Value of Curtailed Energy

High-renewable grids throw away massive amounts of clean energy as curtailment. What if that surplus powered green hydrogen, desalination, or industrial heat? At high CFE thresholds, curtailment represents a billion-dollar opportunity hiding in plain sight.

Revenue Potential by Buyer Type

Four classes of buyer can absorb surplus clean energy at different price points. Green hydrogen electrolyzers are the highest-value off-taker; flexible industrial loads offer the broadest volume absorption.

Curtailment Growth Curve

Curtailment grows exponentially above ~85% CFE threshold as portfolios overbuild renewables to cover the hardest-to-match hours. This surplus is the economic engine behind green hydrogen and power-to-X strategies.

When Does Curtailment Happen?

The temporal shape of curtailment reveals which resources drive it. Solar-heavy regions (CAISO) see midday peaks; wind-heavy regions (SPP, ERCOT) see overnight surplus.

Which Resources Are Curtailed?

Not all curtailment is created equal. Solar curtailment is predictable (midday) and co-locatable with electrolyzers. Wind curtailment is more variable and may require flexible buyers.

Why This Matters

For green hydrogen developers: The hourly curtailment profile tells you exactly when cheap electricity is available. A midday solar surplus in CAISO means your electrolyzer can achieve 30-40% capacity factor on near-zero-cost power — potentially producing hydrogen at $2-3/kg.

For grid planners: Curtailment isn't waste — it's a price signal. Every MWh curtailed is a MWh that could power desalination, district heating, or EV charging at below-wholesale cost. The question isn't whether to curtail, but how to monetize it.

For industrial co-location: Placing energy-intensive industry (data centers, steel, cement) near high-curtailment zones creates a natural sink for surplus energy. The optimal CFE threshold for a region may be higher than the cost-minimum because the curtailed energy has economic value.
