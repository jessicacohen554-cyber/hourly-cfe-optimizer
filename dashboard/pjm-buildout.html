PJM Grid Buildout Comparison

Path-dependent and myopic solver results across cost scenarios, demand levels, and gas dispatch modes — how optimizer structure shapes the 25-year procurement trajectory.

PJM Interconnection serves 65 million people across 13 states with 62.9 GW of existing fossil capacity. Every decarbonization pathway must navigate the same fundamental tension: how aggressively to build clean generation while managing the retirement timeline of a massive thermal fleet. The path-dependent solver optimizes a full 25-year trajectory from 2026 to 2050. The myopic solver optimizes each threshold independently, finding the cheapest mix at 90%, 95%, 99%, and 99.9% clean energy without considering the path between them.

The gas-blind path-dependent solver (gas mode 3) treats gas capacity as a free variable with no shadow price penalty for overbuilding. This creates the upper bound on gas procurement — the most gas-heavy trajectory PJM can follow under base cost assumptions at medium demand growth. The 25-year cumulative system cost reaches $1,787B, with gas capacity peaking at 120 GW before dropping sharply as the target ramps above 95%.

Gas mode 1 (gas-aware dispatch with cost optimization) and mode 2 (gas-aware with enhanced constraints) produce nearly identical trajectories for Pathway A, but diverge for Pathway B where the stranding shadow price interacts differently with each dispatch rule. The myopic solver optimizes each threshold independently — it finds the cheapest resource mix to hit 90%, 95%, 99%, and 99.9% CFE without considering the path between them. This produces cost estimates for each threshold that can't be directly summed into a trajectory (a retroactive cumulative calculation is pending). Pathway B consistently outperforms A across all scenarios, with savings of 20–35% at the 99.9% endpoint. Gas mode 2 (cost-optimized dispatch) beats gas mode 3 (gas-blind) by 10–25% in most configurations.

Cross-Solver Synthesis

Comparing path-dependent and myopic optimizer outcomes

```

The path-dependent and myopic solvers answer fundamentally different questions. The path-dependent solver asks: What's the cheapest 25-year trajectory from here to 99.9% clean? The myopic solver asks: What's the cheapest way to hit each threshold, solved independently? Costs aren't directly comparable — path-dependent cumulative costs include every year of operation, while myopic costs are point-in-time snapshots. The comparison below uses annual system cost from path-dependent results at each threshold's achievement year alongside myopic single-year costs.
