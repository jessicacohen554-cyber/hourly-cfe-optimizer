The 8,760 Problem

Optimal clean energy targets and no-regrets investments across US power markets

Every region has an optimal clean energy target

Grid decarbonization is cheaper than Direct Air Capture — up to a point. That point varies by region and depends on technology costs. The crossover range below shows where each region's marginal abatement cost exceeds projected DAC costs across 5,832 cost scenarios. Within that range, certain resource investments pay off under any plausible cost future.

Key findings across regions

Grid decarbonization beats DAC to at least 69–98% in every region, depending on cost assumptions. SPP and MISO lead with the deepest grid advantages (central crossovers at 96% and 93%), while NEISO and CAISO face the earliest crossovers. The spread is driven by renewable resource quality, existing fleet composition, and firm generation costs.

Regional variation is striking. SPP’s wind dominance keeps grid costs low through 96%, while NEISO’s narrow crossover band (81–82%) reflects its constrained resource base and winter gas pipeline costs. NYISO has the widest uncertainty range (68–98%), making it the most sensitive to cost assumptions. The full crossover range spans from 69% (CAISO under high grid costs) to 98% (ERCOT under conservative DAC assumptions).

The no-regrets investments identified below represent resources that appear in the optimal portfolio across the entire crossover range and under all 5,832 cost sensitivity combinations — they should be built regardless of how cost uncertainty resolves.

Methodology

Marginal Abatement Cost (MAC) Calculation

The MAC curve answers: how much does each additional ton of CO₂ abatement cost via clean energy procurement? We compute it from the cumulative cost and CO₂ abatement data produced by the hourly matching optimizer across 21 decarbonization thresholds (10% to ≥99.99%).

Step 1 — Cumulative supply curve. At each threshold, the optimizer identifies the least-cost portfolio of clean resources (solar, wind, clean firm, CCS, battery, LDES) that achieves hourly carbon-free energy matching. Two cumulative quantities define the abatement supply curve:

  • Cumulative CO₂ abated (metric tons) — total fossil emissions displaced by clean generation at that threshold, computed from 8,760-hour dispatch simulation with merit-order fossil retirement (coal first, then oil, then gas).
  • Cumulative new-build cost ($) — total annualized cost of all new clean resources deployed. Existing clean generation is priced at $0; only new-build resources carry LCOE costs.

Step 2 — PCHIP spline interpolation. The raw data points are inherently noisy because each threshold is independently optimized — the optimizer finds the globally cheapest portfolio at each level without constraining it to build incrementally on prior thresholds. This produces erratic marginal costs when computed as simple Δcost/ΔCO₂ between adjacent points.

To extract the true underlying marginal cost signal, we fit a Piecewise Cubic Hermite Interpolating Polynomial (PCHIP) spline to the cumulative supply curve. PCHIP is chosen over standard cubic splines because it:

  • Preserves the monotonicity of the underlying data (cost and CO₂ both increase with threshold)
  • Avoids overshoot and oscillation artifacts (Runge phenomenon) common with polynomial fits
  • Produces smooth, locally-determined interpolation that respects the physical convexity of the abatement supply curve

Step 3 — Marginal MAC via differentiation. The marginal abatement cost at each threshold is the derivative of the PCHIP spline:

MAC(t) = d(Cost) / d(CO₂)  evaluated at threshold t

This derivative captures the instantaneous rate of cost increase per unit of additional abatement — the true marginal cost of deeper decarbonization at each point along the supply curve.

Step 4 — Isotonic regression. Even after PCHIP smoothing, minor numerical artifacts can produce small non-monotonicities in the derivative. We apply isotonic regression (a constrained optimization that finds the closest non-decreasing function to the raw data in a least-squares sense) to enforce the economic constraint that marginal abatement cost should never decrease as decarbonization deepens. This is the minimum-perturbation correction — it adjusts values only where needed and by the smallest amount possible.

The result is a smooth, monotonically non-decreasing MAC curve that starts low ($50–300/metric ton) where cheap renewables displace high-emitting coal and gas, then rises steeply above 90–95% as expensive firm generation (nuclear, CCS, LDES) is required to fill the last hours of the year.

Sensitivity Bands (P10/P50/P90)

Each region's MAC curve is computed under 5,832 cost sensitivity scenarios formed by crossing 7 toggle groups:

  • 5 paired toggles (Low/Medium/High each): Renewable Generation, Firm Generation, Storage, Fossil Fuel, Transmission
  • CCS cost (Low/Medium/High)
  • 45Q tax credit (On/Off)

The P10 (optimistic), P50 (median), and P90 (pessimistic) bands shown on the charts represent the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles of MAC across all 5,832 scenario evaluations at each threshold. This captures the full range of cost uncertainty — from a world where renewables and firm generation are cheap (P10) to one where costs remain high (P90).

Crossover Analysis

The optimal CFE target for each region is determined by the crossover point where the marginal cost of grid decarbonization exceeds projected Direct Air Capture (DAC) costs. Beyond this point, it becomes cheaper to remove CO₂ directly from the atmosphere than to push the grid further toward hourly matching.

The analysis uses P25/P75 cost bounds (interquartile range) rather than P10/P90 to define the actionable optimal target range. This tighter bound excludes extreme tail scenarios and focuses on the range of outcomes most relevant for investment decisions. The crossover range spans 9 combinations: 3 grid cost tiers (P25/P50/P75) crossed with 3 DAC trajectories (optimistic at ~$150/ton by 2040, central at ~$250/ton, conservative at ~$400/ton). The full P10/P90 uncertainty band remains visible on the interactive charts for reference.

No-Regrets Resource Identification

For each threshold within the crossover range, the optimizer evaluates all 5,832 cost sensitivity scenarios and identifies the cheapest feasible resource portfolio. A resource is classified as:

  • No-regrets (≥80% presence): appears in the optimal portfolio across 80%+ of all scenario-threshold combinations. Build regardless of how costs resolve.
  • Likely (50–80% presence): appears in a majority but not all cost futures.
  • Scenario-dependent (<50% presence): only optimal under specific cost assumptions.

The floor (minimum allocation across all scenarios) represents the absolute minimum investment needed regardless of how costs resolve. The average represents the expected investment level.
