The Lock-In Mechanism

Scarcity pricing and capacity market revenue make gas development rational — until procurement strategy changes the signal.

As clean energy scales from 50% to 95%, wholesale prices collapse — but scarcity events persist. Gas plants earn less revenue per hour, yet the hours they are needed become more valuable. This creates a paradox: gas backup is economically marginal but physically essential, making capacity market payments the primary revenue stream that justifies new gas construction.

Procurement strategy determines whether this dynamic locks in new gas or redirects capital to firm clean alternatives. Strategy 1B (consequential netting) accepts gas as the reliability backstop. Strategy 2C (hourly matching) requires clean generation every hour, pulling forward investment in nuclear, CCS, and long-duration storage that directly displaces gas.

What Replaces Gas

Under hourly matching, firm clean technologies displace gas — and their costs fall with deployment.

Strategy 2C doesn’t just avoid gas — it actively builds the alternatives. Nuclear, CCS-CCGT, and long-duration storage enter the portfolio to fill the hours that solar and wind cannot. Each deployment pushes costs down Wright’s Law learning curves, making the next project cheaper for every buyer.

The FOAK premium for clean firm is real but temporary. At critical mass (~15–25% C&I participation at 90% CFE), learning curves activate and clean firm costs fall below gas true cost within a decade.
