1

The Lock-In Mechanism

Scarcity pricing and capacity market revenue make gas development rational until procurement strategy changes the signal.

As clean energy scales from 50% to 95%, wholesale prices collapse, but scarcity events persist. Gas plants earn less revenue per hour, yet the hours they are needed become more valuable. This creates a structural tension: gas backup is economically marginal but physically essential, making capacity market payments the primary revenue stream that justifies new gas construction.

Procurement strategy determines whether this dynamic locks in new gas or redirects capital to firm clean alternatives. Consequential netting accepts gas as the reliability backstop. Hourly matching requires clean generation every hour, pulling forward investment in nuclear, CCS, and long-duration storage that directly displaces gas.

Consider PJM at 80% clean energy. Wholesale LMP averages roughly $22/MWh—below the operating cost of most peakers. But scarcity hours still reach $200+/MWh for 40–60 hours per year. PJM’s capacity market clears at $270–$333/MW-day (2025/26 auction). At that revenue, a new 500 MW gas CCGT earns $49–$61M/year in capacity payments alone—enough to justify construction if the developer expects 15+ years of operation. Under 1B, that expectation is rational because nothing in the accounting framework penalizes gas backup. Under 2C, corporate buyers paying for hourly-matched clean power create demand for firm clean alternatives that directly compete with that gas plant for capacity revenue.

Once gas gets built, the incentives shift. IPPs with new gas assets have a financial interest in opposing carbon pricing, retirement mandates, and clean energy standards that would strand those investments. The procurement signal corporate buyers send today determines the political coalition that defends or dismantles the fossil fleet tomorrow.

Cross-Company Gas Exposure

How much new gas each IPP develops under market-driven conditions.

New Gas Development by Company (MW)

Vistra and NextEra carry the largest gas development potential under Strategy 1B because their ERCOT and PJM footprints concentrate in the two ISOs where scarcity pricing and capacity payments most strongly reward dispatchable generation. NRG is already gas-heavy, so even modest expansion rates translate to significant new capacity. Constellation’s nuclear fleet gives it minimal gas incentive—its clean firm generation already covers the hours that new gas would serve. Talen’s PJM concentration and datacenter demand pull create a fork: gas backup is the path of least resistance, but firm clean procurement from hyperscaler clients could redirect that capital entirely.

What Hourly Matching Changes

The counterfactual: how does the gas buildout picture change when procurement demands clean generation every hour?

High PPA market depth doesn't just avoid gas. It actively builds the alternatives. Nuclear, CCS-CCGT, and long-duration storage enter the portfolio to fill the hours that solar and wind cannot. Each deployment pushes costs down Wright's Law learning curves, making the next project cheaper for every buyer.

The FOAK premium for clean firm is real but temporary. At critical mass (~15-25% C&I participation at 90% CFE), learning curves activate and clean firm costs fall below gas true cost within a decade.
