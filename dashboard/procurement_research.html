4 Grid Average vs. Fossil Average vs. Marginal Emission Rates

The choice of emission rate baseline determines how much CO₂ a buyer needs to "offset" under consequential accounting. Three different baselines produce three different procurement strategies.

tCO₂/MWh		 Fossil Avg (B)

CAISO 0.168 0.392 0.397 Gas CCGT/CT C ≈ B
ERCOT 0.333 0.535 0.526 Gas + coal mix C ≈ B
PJM 0.325 0.539 0.573 Coal + gas mix C > B
MISO 0.354 0.567 0.663 Coal + gas mix C >> B (+17%)
NYISO 0.217 0.415 0.437 Gas CCGT/CT C ≈ B
NEISO 0.246 0.387 0.425 Gas CCGT/CT C slightly > B
SPP 0.340 0.544 0.665 Coal + gas mix C >> B (+22%)
Marginal > Fossil Average in coal-heavy ISOs. In MISO (+17%) and SPP (+22%), the short-run marginal emission rate significantly exceeds the fossil fleet average because coal plants cycling at low utilization have degraded thermal efficiency. In gas-dominated ISOs (CAISO, NYISO, NEISO), marginal ≈ fossil average because gas is both average and marginal.

Implication for Strategy 1C: Netting against marginal rates means buyers in MISO/SPP face a higher starting bar to reach "zero" — but each MWh of clean energy procured also displaces more CO₂, so the $/tCO₂ may still be favorable if cheap wind is available.

5 Procurement Strategy Taxonomy

Three families of strategies, each with sub-variants that define what counts as "clean" and how matching works.

Strategy 1 — Consequential Cross-Regional Netting

Buyers purchase the cheapest $/tCO₂ clean energy anywhere in the US to "net" against their location-based carbon emissions. Requires new build or nuclear uprates. No ISO boundary constraint.

1A

Grid-Average Baseline

Buyer starts from their ISO's grid-average emission rate (includes clean energy in the denominator). Lowest starting bar. A CA buyer with 0.168 tCO₂/MWh can reach "zero" cheaply by buying MISO wind that displaces coal at ~1.0 tCO₂/MWh.

1B

Fossil-Average Baseline

Buyer starts from their ISO's fossil-only average emission rate. Higher bar — you're betting against the fossil fleet's average intensity. CA buyer faces 0.392 vs 0.168 in 1A.

1C

Marginal Emission Baseline

Buyer starts from the short-run marginal emission rate. Potentially highest bar in coal-heavy ISOs (MISO: 0.663 vs fossil avg 0.567). Optional — depends on whether marginal > fossil avg is material enough to model.

Strategy 2 — Hourly Matching (Same-ISO)

Buyer matches load hour-by-hour within their own ISO. No cross-regional procurement. Highest verifiability but highest cost.

2A

Pure New Build

100% new build requirement. No credit for existing grid clean. Most expensive; maximum additionality. This is the existing Track 2 NB analysis.

2B

Existing Clean Baseline + New Build

Buyer takes credit for existing clean grid mix as baseline, then procures new build on top. Reduces cost significantly in clean-grid ISOs (CAISO, NYISO, NEISO).

2C

Pro-Rata Allocation + Premium Existing + New Build

Pro-rata share of RPS/nuclear/public utility clean allocated to each buyer. Premium for existing clean to keep it online (cost-to-replace). New build on top. Premium TBD.

Strategy 3 — Annual Matching

Volumetric annual matching without hourly temporal constraint. 2×2 matrix: {Same-ISO, Cross-Regional} × {Additionality Required, No Additionality}.

3A

Same-ISO + New Build

Annual matching within buyer's ISO. Only new-build clean energy counts. Comparable to Strategy 2A but without hourly granularity.

3B

Cross-Regional + New Build

Annual matching from any US ISO. Only new-build clean counts. Comparable to Strategy 1 but annual volumetric rather than consequential netting.

3C

Same-ISO + No Additionality

Annual matching within buyer's ISO. Existing clean counts (includes unbundled RECs from existing generators).

3D

Cross-Regional + No Additionality

Annual matching from any US ISO. Existing clean counts. Cheapest option — unbundled RECs from anywhere. This is the "status quo" for most corporate procurement today.

Cross-Cutting Layer: FOAK-to-NOAK Learning Curves (2ABC-i)

An optional layer applicable to any Strategy 2 variant that incorporates first-of-a-kind to Nth-of-a-kind cost learning for clean firm resources. Prioritizes cheapest $/tCO₂ queueing while reflecting the deployment-driven cost reductions modeled in the existing Scenario A/B consequential analysis.

6 Dashboard Concept

Hybrid scrollytelling + interactive comparison tool. Integrates tradeoff matrix and strategy horse race elements.

Page Structure (Decided)

  1. Scrollytell intro: Explains the three strategy families, builds intuition about tradeoffs
  2. Tradeoff matrix: All strategies × key metrics (cost, CO₂, build, $/tCO₂) — embedded in scrollytell
  3. Strategy horse race: Fixed outcome ("to achieve 80% clean: cheapest strategy?") + fixed budget ("with $X/MWh: what does each achieve?")
  4. Interactive section: Strategy selector, participation sliders, CFE threshold, ISO selector
  5. System-wide panel: CO₂ reduction target → required participation by strategy

Controls (Decided)

  • Strategy selector: Toggle between 1A/1B/1C, 2A/2B/2C, 3A/3B/3C/3D (and compare any 2-3 side by side)
  • Hyperscaler participation slider: % of C&I load from top ~30 hyperscaler/tech buyers
  • Other corporate participation slider: % of remaining C&I load from mid-market buyers
  • Per-buyer CFE target: CFE threshold slider (parallels main dashboard)
  • ISO selector: Per-region or all-region view

Outputs

  • Total cost ($/year and $/MWh premium)
  • CO₂ displaced (mtCO₂/year)
  • Resource build required (GW by type)
  • $/tCO₂ abated (MAC)
  • Cross-strategy comparison for equivalent outcomes
  • System-wide: required participation to achieve target CO₂ reduction by strategy

8 Open Design Questions

Decisions needed before implementation can proceed.

Decided

  • Strategy 3 sub-variants → 2×2 matrix: 3A/3B/3C/3D (boundary × additionality)
  • Participation slider → Dual national sliders: hyperscaler + all other corporate
  • Emissions reduction → Dual-mode: per-buyer CFE target + system-wide CO₂ reduction panel
  • Dashboard framing → Hybrid scrollytell with integrated tradeoff matrix + strategy horse race

Remaining

  1. Cost-to-replace premium (Strategy 2C): What premium to keep existing clean online? How to balance existing vs. new build consumption order?
  2. Strategy 1C inclusion: Marginal > fossil average is material in MISO (+17%) and SPP (+22%) but negligible in gas-dominated ISOs. Include or skip?
  3. FOAK-to-NOAK layer: Apply same learning curve logic from Scenario A/B to hourly matching strategies? Does deployment sequencing matter across ISOs?
  4. Hyperscaler vs. other split: What % of C&I load is "hyperscaler"? Need a defensible estimate (BNEF data suggests ~10-15% of C&I and growing rapidly).
  5. System-wide panel mechanics: When backing into required participation, how does the model handle supply constraints (e.g., NEISO can't source enough clean for 100% hourly matching)?
