Wright's Law Learning Curves

How collective procurement drives clean energy cost declines

The Critical Mass Question

Wright's Law predicts that technology costs decline by a fixed percentage each time cumulative production doubles. Solar panels followed this pattern — falling 99% since 1976. But clean firm generation (advanced nuclear, CCS-CCGT) hasn't hit the first doubling yet. The question isn't whether costs will fall, but when enough buyers commit to trigger the learning curve.

This analysis models what happens when C&I electricity buyers collectively adopt hourly CFE matching (Strategy 2C) at different participation levels. Below a critical mass threshold, new-build clean firm stays at FOAK pricing. Above it, Wright's Law activates and costs plummet toward NOAK levels — benefiting every participant.

Technology Learning Curves: FOAK to NOAK

Wright's Law cost trajectories for CCS-CCGT and advanced nuclear, gated by critical mass. Below the first-doubling threshold (8 GW CCS, 5 GW nuclear), costs stay at FOAK levels. After critical mass, they follow the standard learning curve toward NOAK pricing.

The doubling threshold matters. CCS-CCGT needs ~8 GW of global deployment to trigger its learning curve (down from $--/MWh to $--/MWh). Nuclear needs ~5 GW (down from $--/MWh to $--/MWh). At 25% C&I participation targeting 90% CFE, both thresholds are crossed.

Critical Mass: Participation Required by CFE Threshold

Higher CFE targets require more clean firm generation per participant, meaning fewer participants are needed to collectively cross the first-doubling threshold. At 99% CFE, just 5% participation triggers learning. At 90% CFE, it takes 25%.

Ambitious targets are self-reinforcing. The companies that set the highest CFE targets contribute the most new-build capacity per MWh of demand — reaching critical mass faster and unlocking cost declines for everyone, including latecomers at lower thresholds.

Per-ISO Spend Decomposition at 90% CFE

Where the money goes: existing clean premium payments vs. new-build investment, by ISO. ISOs with large existing nuclear fleets (PJM, NYISO, NEISO) have high premium shares. ISOs with less existing clean (ERCOT, SPP) are predominantly new-build.

PJM is the new-build engine. With 843 TWh of demand, even modest participation in PJM generates massive new-build volume — the single largest contributor to reaching Wright's Law critical mass globally.
