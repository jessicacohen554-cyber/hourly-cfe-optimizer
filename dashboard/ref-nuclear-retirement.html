Cross-Model Comparison

Where do the models agree on plant outcomes, and where do they diverge?

Cross-Model Comparison: Where Do the Models Agree? Synthesis

When all three models classify a plant the same way, the finding is structural — it holds regardless of which assumptions you believe about gas prices, demand growth, or transition speed. These plants are either robustly safe or robustly at risk under any plausible future.

When models disagree, the divergence itself is the insight. Model A and B track current market trajectories; Model C captures the price cannibalization that only appears at high clean penetration. Plants that are safe in A/B but stranded in C face a conditional risk: their fate depends entirely on how fast renewables suppress wholesale prices.

Under Which Futures? — Net-Zero Pathway Analysis (10 Scenarios)Structured transition trajectories: power-sector NZ, economy-wide NZ, queue-driven, and reference Model B

Instead of parametric sweeps, this model tests specific, named transition futures. The key insight to watch for: economy-wide net-zero (AT3/AT4) produces higher LMPs than power-sector-only NZ (AT1/AT2) because electrification-driven demand growth partially offsets renewable price suppression. For nuclear owners, the worst future isn't maximum decarbonization — it's decarbonization of the power sector alone without offsetting demand growth.

Revenue Waterfall & Policy Safety Net

State CfD programs and the federal 45U PTC act as revenue floors — but the policy cliff is steep.

Revenue Waterfall & Policy Safety Net All Models

State CfD programs and the federal 45U PTC act as revenue floors: they fill the gap between market revenue and a guaranteed minimum, but don't inflate profits for already-profitable plants. The policy cliff is steep — IL CEJA expires 2027, NY ZEC by 2029, and the federal 45U PTC lapses in 2032. By 2033, every merchant nuclear plant faces fully unhedged market exposure. This timeline is critical context for the clean firm resource analysis: if nuclear retires, the clean firm replacement cost becomes the floor for achieving the same carbon-free output.
