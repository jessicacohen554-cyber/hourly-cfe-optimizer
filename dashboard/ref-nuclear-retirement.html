Merchant Nuclear Stranding Risk in the Clean Energy Transition
Three independent modeling approaches — parametric market sweeps, structured net-zero pathways, and cost-optimized baselines — stress-test the IPP nuclear fleet under hundreds of scenarios to identify which risks are real, which are model artifacts, and which findings hold universally.
Cross-Model Synthesis

The Clean Asset Value analysis shows wholesale prices falling below nuclear viability at 65–75% clean energy. This page asks: which specific plants, under which specific futures, at which specific thresholds? Three independent models stress-test 25 merchant nuclear plants to separate robust findings from model artifacts — and quantify the fleet at risk if corporate procurement signals don't evolve.

Why Three Models?

No single model captures the full picture of nuclear stranding risk. Each asks a fundamentally different question. When all three agree a plant is at risk, that's a robust finding. When they disagree, it reveals which assumptions are driving the result.

Step 10 Reference

“How likely is stranding?”

Model A — Probabilistic Stress Test

  • 270 parametric scenarios: market conditions × demand growth × price sensitivity × PPA levels × gas friction
  • Turns many knobs simultaneously to map the probability distribution of stranding risk
  • LMP and clean penetration evolve endogenously per scenario

Answers: Across a wide range of plausible futures, what percentage of scenarios push this plant below viability?

Step 10 NZ Pathways

“Under which transition futures?”

Model B — Net-Zero Pathway Analysis

  • 10 structured scenarios: Power Sector NZ (AT1/AT2), Economy-Wide NZ (AT3/AT4), Queue-Driven (QT1–4), Reference (R1/R2)
  • Each defines a specific ISO-level LMP and clean penetration trajectory through 2050
  • Tests whether decarbonization scope matters: power-sector-only vs. economy-wide

Answers: If the grid follows a net-zero trajectory, which specific pathway strands which plants — and does the scope of decarbonization matter?

Step 3A Track 1

“At what clean energy threshold?”

Model C — Price Cannibalization Mechanics

  • Derived from 5,832-scenario cost optimization across clean penetration thresholds 50%–99%
  • Captures the core mechanism: as clean energy grows, wholesale prices fall, eroding nuclear merchant revenue
  • Isolates the price cannibalization effect from other market dynamics

Answers: At what percentage of clean energy does the wholesale price cross below this plant's viability floor — unless policy or procurement signals intervene?

Cross-Model Comparison: Where Do the Models Agree? Synthesis

When all three models agree a plant is at risk — Model A says stranding is probable (>50% of scenarios), Model B says it happens under both power-sector and economy-wide NZ, and Model C says the cannibalization threshold is within the planning horizon — that plant faces structural stranding risk that isn't sensitive to modeling assumptions.

When models disagree, it reveals which assumptions matter: if Model A shows high risk but Model B doesn't, the risk depends on market volatility rather than the transition pathway. If Model B shows risk under power-sector NZ but not economy-wide, the pace and scope of decarbonization matter more than the destination. If Model C shows a low threshold but Model A doesn't, capacity market revenue or policy support may be cushioning the blow.

Revenue Waterfall & Policy Safety Net All Models

State CfD programs and the federal 45U PTC act as temporary revenue floors: they fill the gap between market revenue and a guaranteed minimum, but don't inflate profits for already-profitable plants. Most state programs expire by 2029; the federal 45U PTC expires in 2032.

After 2032, the fleet's survival depends entirely on market revenue and corporate PPA demand — the same procurement signal analyzed on the homepage. If wholesale prices follow Model C's cannibalization trajectory, the absence of policy support means pure merchant exposure. The Clean Firm analysis shows that replacing retired nuclear costs 3–5× the wholesale price that caused the retirement.
