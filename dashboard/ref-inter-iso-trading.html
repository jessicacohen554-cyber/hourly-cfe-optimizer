Reference / Inter-ISO Energy Trading

The Case for Cross-Regional Clean Energy Trading

CAISO has midday solar surplus while PJM needs evening power. SPP's wind blows strongest when NEISO's is calm. Cross-regional hourly netting could cut clean energy procurement costs significantly — if transmission economics allow it.

Island vs. Pool Cost Comparison

When each ISO procures clean energy independently ("island"), costs vary widely based on local resource quality. A pooled market where ISOs trade surplus energy reduces costs for high-cost regions by sourcing from cheaper neighbors.

Net Position: Exporters vs. Importers

Regions with abundant cheap renewables (SPP wind, ERCOT solar+wind) become net exporters. Resource-constrained regions (NEISO, NYISO) benefit most as importers — accessing clean energy they can't produce domestically at competitive cost.

Trading Pair Values

The most valuable trading pairs combine low-cost supply with proximity (low transmission cost). SPP → PJM and ERCOT → MISO consistently rank highest due to cheap wind resources and moderate inter-regional distance.

Savings by Threshold

Trading value increases at higher CFE thresholds because the "last mile" of clean energy is the most expensive — and the most likely to be available cheaply in a neighboring region.

Transmission Cost Sensitivity

How much does inter-regional transmission cost affect the value of trading? Even at high TX costs ($20+/MWh), trading remains valuable at extreme thresholds where the alternative is expensive local last-mile procurement.

Why This Matters

For policy makers: Inter-regional clean energy trading could save $5-15B annually across the US grid. New HVDC transmission corridors — especially SPP-to-East and CAISO-to-Southwest — have the highest benefit-cost ratios for enabling this trade.

For corporate buyers: Cross-regional procurement (via VPPAs or EAC markets) can reduce your 24/7 CFE costs by 10-20% compared to same-ISO-only strategies. The key is finding trading partners with complementary generation profiles — not just the cheapest spot price.

For market designers: Regional clean energy attribute markets (hourly EACs) need to be interoperable across ISOs for this value to be captured. Current market fragmentation — different tracking systems, different granularity — prevents efficient cross-regional clean energy trade.
