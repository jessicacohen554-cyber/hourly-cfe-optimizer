Key Findings

What the data reveals about procurement strategy choice

—

Strategies look identical at low adoption

Cost spreads are negligible below 20% C&I participation. The divergence begins where most corporate buyers haven't looked yet.

—

Hourly matching builds the grid you need

Only hourly strategies force investment in firm clean generation and long-duration storage — the resources a deeply decarbonized grid requires.

—

Cheap today, expensive tomorrow

Consequential and annual strategies defer learning-curve investment. By 2040, they pay near-FOAK prices for technology that hourly buyers unlocked at NOAK a decade earlier.

The Debate Within Hourly
If hourly matching is the most robust family, the next question is: which version? The differences are larger than you'd think.
Figure 7 — Strategy 2 Internal Comparison

The Stranding Paradox: 2A vs 2B vs 2C

Six dimensions compared across the three hourly matching variants. The variant that sounds safest (2B) has the worst failure mode.

Dimension 2A — All New 2B — Grid Baseline 2C — Premium + New
Wholesale erosion Accelerates locally Moderate Revenue floor mitigates
Existing clean stranding Ignores — no help, no harm Worst — claims credit without paying Addressed — premium keeps plants online
Learning curve signal Strong — maximum new deployment Diluted — less new build needed Strong — new build layered on top
Cost trajectory Highest near-term cost Lowest — free-rides on existing Moderate — premium + new build
Firm investment signal Builds firm + storage from day one Reduces requirement for firm Preserves existing + builds new firm
Additionality 100% additional by definition Reduced — existing clean offsets requirement Existing preserved + new added on top

Strategy 2B is arguably the worst hourly variant — it actively takes credit for existing clean generation without directing any revenue to those generators. When unpaid existing generation retires, 2B buyers face the same replacement cost spike as everyone else.

Figure 8 — Critical Mass Threshold

Strategy 2C Has the Opposite Problem

Every other strategy degrades as adoption increases. Strategy 2C needs enough adoption to work — below a critical mass, you're just paying premiums. Above it, you've funded the learning curve.

Maintenance Mode

Below critical mass. Premium spend dominates. Not enough new-build volume to trigger learning curve.

Learning Activated

Past critical mass. Aggregate new-build firm hits first Wright's Law doubling. NOAK pricing begins. Cost curve bends down.

The critical mass threshold is lower than you'd think — because learning is global. A nuclear plant built in PJM drives NOAK pricing for nuclear in NEISO. All 7 ISOs contribute to the same global learning pool.

Figure 9 — Regional Investment Roles

Different Regions Play Different Roles Under Strategy 2C

Nuclear-heavy ISOs are the premium-payers — their spend keeps existing clean alive. Renewable-rich ISOs are the learning-curve drivers. Both are essential.

Each region contributes differently to the same NOAK outcome. Regions with large existing clean fleets pay premiums to prevent stranding. Regions with strong renewable resources deploy the new capacity that drives the learning curve.
