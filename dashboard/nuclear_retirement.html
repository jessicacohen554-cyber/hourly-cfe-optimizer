Nuclear Retirement Risk Analysis

Generator Analysis Policy & Markets March 2026
Key finding: The IPP nuclear fleet is broadly profitable on merchant economics alone across most market scenarios, thanks primarily to PJM's strong capacity market ($120/kW-yr). However, this analysis reveals a critical asymmetry: state CfD programs and the federal 45U PTC function as revenue floors — they protect against downside scenarios but don't inflate returns for already-profitable plants. The real risk emerges post-2032 when both the 45U PTC and most state programs will have expired, leaving plants fully exposed to merchant markets.

State Nuclear Policy Landscape

State programs and the federal 45U PTC operate as contracts-for-difference (CfD): they guarantee a revenue floor, paying only the shortfall between market revenue and the floor price. They do not stack — whichever floor is higher applies. Plants already profitable above the floor receive no CfD payment.

45U PTC Expiration Impact

The 45U PTC provides a $15/MWh revenue floor to all existing nuclear through 2032. At $4.55B/yr across the fleet, its expiration is the single largest cliff risk — but most plants don't currently need it because market revenue already exceeds the floor.

The CfD paradox: When market conditions are good enough that plants don't need the floor, the floor's existence doesn't matter. When conditions deteriorate enough to activate the floor, that's precisely when political support for renewal is weakest (higher electricity prices = less public appetite for nuclear subsidies).
