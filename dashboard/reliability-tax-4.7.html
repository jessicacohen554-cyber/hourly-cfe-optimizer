The Reliability Tax

What ratepayers pay when wind, solar, and storage are asked to cover reliability on their own.
v4.7

Wind, solar, and batteries can deliver clean energy. They cannot, on their own, cover the worst hour of the worst week — the hour grid planners are legally obligated to keep the lights on for. Something has to stand behind them. Absent new clean firm generation, that something is gas. The capex, fixed costs, and eventual write-off of that backup fleet land on customer bills. That’s the reliability tax.

Why a clean-only grid still builds gas

As clean-energy targets climb, the grid doesn’t need more firm capacity — it needs firm capacity used less. An expensive shape to buy.

70% clean
Moderate solar. Gentle net-load curve. Firm gas still runs a broad share of hours.
95% clean
Enough solar to saturate midday (curtailment). Same sharp evening peak — still gas territory.
Demand (load) Solar dispatched Residual gap (gas territory)

Between 70% and 95% clean, solar and storage scale faster than load does. Midday fills in — often overshoots, forcing curtailment — but the evening peak barely moves. The gap firm capacity covers doesn’t shrink evenly. It gets narrower and sharper. A few hundred hours a year still demand something that burns fuel, and those hours land in a tighter, pricier window.

New gas sizing runs off hourly dispatch, not a thumbnail capacity credit. Flat credits — 30% for solar, 95% for batteries — smooth over the worst week and systematically under-build. Gas is sized instead against the hour that 8,757 others exceed (about 2.6 hours of tail risk a year), which is the bar planners actually have to clear.

Each panel is one grid, same axes. On the wind-and-solar paths, the curve climbs, peaks, and then falls as clean-energy targets rise past 90%: gas built to cover residual peaks gets unwound when storage and curtailment finally close the gap. The clean-firm path sits lower and flatter — gas sized to the worst hour once, and left there.

Peak build-out lands in the middle, not the end. On the wind-and-solar path, ERCOT peaks at 134 GW of new gas near 80% clean, PJM peaks at 106 GW near 80%, MISO peaks at 70 GW near 90%. Wind-rich and hydro-heavy grids — NEISO, NYISO, SPP — stay in single-digit to low-tens-of-GW territory. The clean-firm path peaks earlier and lower in every grid, and never climbs as high.
