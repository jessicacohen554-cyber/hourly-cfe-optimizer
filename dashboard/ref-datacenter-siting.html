Where Should You Build Your Data Center?

A physics-based comparison of hourly clean energy costs across 7 US electricity markets. For hyperscalers pursuing 24/7 carbon-free energy matching, location choice can mean a 2-3x difference in procurement cost.

Regional Rankings

Cost Curves Across All Thresholds

The cost of hourly CFE matching rises nonlinearly with target ambition. Regions with abundant wind (SPP, ERCOT, MISO) maintain cost advantages throughout. Regions dependent on clean firm power (NEISO, NYISO) face steeper cost curves past 90%.

Resource Mix by Region

What you need to buy depends on where you build. Wind-rich regions (SPP, ERCOT) need minimal clean firm. Solar-rich regions (CAISO) face duck-curve challenges requiring storage. Eastern ISOs (PJM, NYISO, NEISO) lean on existing nuclear and CCS.

The siting decision is a 2.5x cost multiplier. At 90% hourly CFE, the cheapest region costs ~$23/MWh while the most expensive costs ~$58/MWh. At 99%, the spread widens further. For a 100 MW data center, that's a $30M+/year difference in energy procurement costs.

Considerations Beyond Cost

Fiber connectivity: Northern Virginia (PJM) and the Dallas-Fort Worth area (ERCOT) have the highest data center density and fiber capacity. Low energy cost ISOs like SPP have less established data center infrastructure.

Water availability: Data center cooling requires significant water. Western ISOs face drought constraints. Eastern regions generally have better water access.

Grid reliability: ERCOT's isolated grid has experienced extreme weather disruptions. PJM and MISO have stronger interconnections. CAISO faces wildfire-related outage risks.

Tax incentives: State-level incentives (tax abatements, renewable energy credits, enterprise zones) vary significantly and can shift the economic calculus by $3-5/MWh.
