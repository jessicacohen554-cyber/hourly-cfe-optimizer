Efficiency Determines Dispatch Priority

The fleet's most efficient CCGTs (HR < 6.5 MMBtu/MWh) maintain capacity factors above 70% across nearly all scenarios, while legacy units (HR > 9.0) face economic retirement under high-renewable-penetration conditions. The spread between the most and least efficient tiers widens after 2030 as renewable capacity displaces marginal gas generation.

Gas Friction & Demand Growth Drive the Most Uncertainty

Fleet emissions are most sensitive to the gas friction dimension (pipeline constraints, fuel cost volatility) and demand growth rates. Under high gas friction + high demand, the fleet retains higher capacity factors and emissions persist longer. Under low friction + low demand, economic retirements accelerate and emissions decline faster than SBTi trajectories require.

Location Shapes Plant-Level Outcomes

ERCOT plants face the steepest CF declines due to aggressive solar + wind buildout, while PJM plants benefit from capacity market revenue that sustains dispatch even as energy margins shrink. CAISO plants with high heat rates are the most retirement-vulnerable — the combination of solar surplus and transmission constraints creates persistent negative margins during peak hours.

Retirement Risk Concentrates in the Tail

Under P90 (pessimistic) conditions, most legacy CCGTs remain operational through 2050. But under P10 (optimistic) conditions, economic retirements could reduce the fossil fleet by 30–40% by 2040. The transition is not gradual — it concentrates in 2028–2035 when renewable additions cross regional tipping points.
