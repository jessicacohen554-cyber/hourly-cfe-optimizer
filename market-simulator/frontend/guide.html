User Guide

Market Simulation Tool

A screening tool for directionally correct analysis of how grid assets perform under different market conditions. Use this to identify sensitivity ranges worth exploring deeper with IPM or GenX capacity models.

What This Tool Does

This simulator models electricity market economics across 7 US ISOs (CAISO, ERCOT, PJM, NYISO, NEISO, MISO, SPP). Given a set of market preconditions — fuel prices, carbon costs, clean energy LCOEs, tax incentives, and demand growth — it computes merit-order dispatch, hourly LMPs, generator profitability, and clean energy deployment economics.

It answers: Under these cost assumptions, what happens to the fossil fleet? Which generators retire? What clean resources can be profitably deployed? At what carbon price does CCS break even?

Note: This is a screening tool producing directionally correct results, not a production capacity expansion model. Use it to narrow the parameter space before running full IPM or GenX analyses.

How to Use It

  1. Choose a simulation mode Snapshot runs a single point-in-time simulation (fast, ~5-15 seconds). Trajectory projects forward through multiple years with learning curves. Sweep runs 270 scenarios across all parameter combinations.
  2. Select ISO region(s) Pick one ISO for Snapshot mode, or multiple ISOs for Trajectory/Sweep. Each ISO has different demand profiles, fossil fleet composition, and capacity market structures.
  3. Set market parameters Adjust fuel prices, carbon price, clean energy LCOEs, federal incentives, storage costs, and more. Each input has an ⓘ info icon — hover over it to see what the parameter controls and typical ranges. Use L/M/H preset buttons for quick selection, or enter any custom value.
  4. Click "Run Market Simulation" The model will compute results (typically 5-30 seconds for Snapshot/Trajectory). You'll be redirected to the results page automatically.
  5. Review results The results page shows KPI summary cards, LMP profiles, supply stack, merit-order dispatch, generator economics, and deployment profitability. Each chart has an info hover explaining what it shows.
  6. Export & compare Each simulation run is automatically saved to the results/ directory with a unique ID. You'll find CSV data files, chart images, a narrative summary, and an HTML snapshot of the results page.

Interpreting Results

Top-Level KPI Cards

Market Clean %

The percentage of generation from clean sources after the model computes economic dispatch. Higher = more fossil retirement.

Avg LMP ($/MWh)

Time-weighted average locational marginal price. Driven by the marginal fossil generator's cost — as clean penetration rises, this typically falls.

Annual Emissions (MT CO2)

Total annual CO2 emissions from the fossil fleet. Drops as clean generation displaces coal and gas.

Nuclear Revenue ($/MWh)

Total revenue stack for existing nuclear: energy + capacity + 45U PTC. If this falls below the retirement threshold, nuclear plants shut down.

Charts & Visualizations

LMP & Capacity Revenue

Hourly price profile (Snapshot) or year-over-year average LMP trajectory. Shows how energy prices evolve as the generation mix changes.

Supply Stack

Generation mix by fuel source (TWh). Shows the shift from fossil to clean resources under your assumptions.

Merit-Order Stack

Fossil generators ordered by marginal cost ($/MWh). Generators above the LMP line are uneconomic and face retirement pressure.

Generator Economics Table

Per-unit economics: capacity, dispatch hours, capacity factor, revenue, cost, and profit margin. Color-coded status (profitable / marginal / retiring).

Generator Profitability

Revenue vs. cost per generator class (Trajectory mode). Shows which resources are profitable to deploy at each projection year.

CCS Retrofit vs. New Gas

Carbon price crossover analysis. Shows the carbon price at which CCS-CCGT becomes cheaper than unabated gas.

Custom Data Inputs

You can override the model's built-in price curves with your own time-series data. Custom input files go in the custom-user-inputs/ folder inside the market-simulator directory.

Available Templates

Template FileFormatWhat It Overrides
template_lmp_hourly.csv 8760 rows × 7 ISO columns ($/MWh) Hourly LMP price curve per ISO
template_capacity_prices.csv 12 rows (months) × 7 ISO columns ($/MW-day) Monthly capacity market prices per ISO
template_rec_prices.csv 12 rows (months) × 7 ISO columns ($/MWh) Monthly REC prices per ISO
template_fuel_prices_gas.csv 12 rows (months) × 7 ISO columns ($/MMBtu) Monthly natural gas prices by region
template_fuel_prices_coal.csv 12 rows (months) × 7 ISO columns ($/MMBtu) Monthly coal prices by region
template_fuel_prices_oil.csv 12 rows (months) × 7 ISO columns ($/MMBtu) Monthly oil prices by region

How to Use Custom Files

  1. Open the template CSV Find the template in custom-user-inputs/. Each file is pre-filled with the model's default values.
  2. Replace values with your data Keep the header row and column structure. Fill in your price data. Leave ISOs you don't need as-is.
  3. Save as the same filename Rename the template by removing template_ prefix (e.g., lmp_hourly.csv). Save in the same folder.
  4. Enable on the Setup page On the simulation setup page, check the "Use custom file" toggle for the relevant input category. The tool will show whether the file was found and validated.

Important: Custom files must match the exact column headers (ISO names) and row counts shown in the templates. Files with missing columns, wrong row counts, or NaN values will be rejected and the model will fall back to defaults.
