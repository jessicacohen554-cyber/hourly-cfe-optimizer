User Guide

Market Simulation Tool

A fossil dispatch simulator that evaluates what happens to the incumbent fleet across thousands of market scenarios. Shows which findings hold across a wide range of assumptions, not just one assumed future.

Getting Started

The simulator runs out of the box with built-in synthetic profiles. For production-quality results, drop real data files into the data/ folder before launching.

Data quality matters: On the Minimal (demo) tier, the model uses synthetic profiles — results are illustrative only, suitable for exploring the UI but not for screening decisions. The Setup page shows color-coded data tier indicators (green/amber/red) so you can see exactly which data sources are active. The Results page shows a warning banner when running on synthetic data.

Emission & Dispatch Data Model

Without EIA plant data (Minimal/EIA-930 tiers): The model uses fleet-average heat rates per unit type (coal: 10.0, CCGT: 7.0, CT: 10.5 MMBtu/MWh) and fleet-average CO2 rates from EPA eGRID. All coal plants share one heat rate; all CCGTs share one. Generator economics are reported per unit type (coal_steam, gas_ccgt, gas_ct, oil_ct), not per plant.

With EIA-860/923/CAMPD data (Full fidelity): Each plant uses its own reported heat rate, CO2 emission rate, and online year. Unit commitment parameters are vintage-adjusted — newer CCGTs (2015+) turn down to 30% vs 50% for pre-2005 units, with lower start-up costs. Plant-level results include per-plant capacity factor, dispatch hours, revenue, fuel cost, profit, and status (operating/at-risk/stranded).

Data Directory Format

EIA-930 Profiles: Place hourly profiles (parquet or JSON) in data/profiles/ or data/eia-930/. The tool auto-detects both formats. Files: eia_demand_profiles, eia_gen_profiles (or eia_generation_profiles), eia_fossil_mix, egrid_emission_rates. Generation profiles must be normalized shape arrays (sum ≈ 1.0 per resource), not raw capacity factors.

Pipeline Parquets: Copy step_2_1_ef_{ISO}.parquet into data/step2.1-ef/ and step_2_2a_CO_{ISO}.parquet into data/step2.2-cost/. ISOs: CAISO, ERCOT, PJM, NYISO, NEISO, MISO, SPP. Without these, the tool generates synthetic resource mix data for screening-level analysis.

EIA-860 (plant characteristics): One JSON per state: data/eia-860/TX/eia860_TX.json. Must include fields: plant_id, plant_name, state, capacity_mw, fuel_type, prime_mover, year_built, heat_rate, balancing_authority_code, status. The balancing_authority_code field is critical — it maps plants to ISOs (e.g., ERCO → ERCOT, CISO → CAISO). Plants are classified by prime mover + fuel type (e.g., CA/CT/CC → gas_ccgt, ST + BIT/SUB → coal_steam).

EIA-923 (monthly generation): Create one subfolder per state: data/eia-923/TX/. Inside, place monthly JSON files named eia923_TX_2024-06.json. Must include: plant_id, plant_name, fuel_type, net_generation_mwh, year, month. Provides revealed heat rates and capacity factors per plant.

EPA CAMPD (hourly emissions): Same state subfolder structure: data/epa-campd/TX/. Files named TX_2024-06.json (no prefix). Must include: orispl_code, unit_id, op_date, op_hour, co2_mass_tons, nox_mass_lbs, so2_mass_lbs, gross_load_mw. Provides actual stack-level emission rates.

What This Tool Does

This is a fossil dispatch overlay on exogenous clean generation shapes. The upstream optimizer pipeline generates physics-feasible clean energy mixes and optimizes their costs. This tool takes those clean generation profiles, subtracts them from demand to derive residual fossil load, and dispatches the fossil fleet against that residual across 7 US ISOs (CAISO, ERCOT, PJM, NYISO, NEISO, MISO, SPP).

It answers: Given this clean generation backdrop, what happens to the fossil fleet? Which generators retire? What are the LMP and emissions consequences? At what carbon price does CCS break even?

The 1,215-scenario parametric sweep evaluates these questions across thousands of plausible futures (fuel prices × carbon prices × demand growth × PPA availability × gas friction × interconnection caps), delivering P10/P50/P90 distributions. This shows which findings hold across a wide range of input assumptions rather than a single "optimal" answer contingent on getting every assumption right.

Advanced Modeling Features

ORDC Scarcity Pricing

Operating Reserve Demand Curve replaces statistical price overlays with structural reserve-based pricing. LOLP sigmoid models probability of lost load as reserves tighten. ISO-calibrated VOLL ($2K–$5K/MWh) produces realistic scarcity spikes.

VRE Cannibalization

Solar/wind energy revenue uses time-matched LMP (capture rates), not average LMP. Solar capture rates fall below 1.0 as penetration increases — the duck curve effect. ORDC scarcity hours create a revenue floor. This prevents the model from overestimating VRE deployment at high penetration.

Zonal LMP Decomposition

Pipe-and-bubble zonal model with 2–5 zones per ISO, inter-zonal transfer limits, and ORDC-integrated scarcity pricing. Captures 60–80% of real congestion effects. Automatically enabled when fleet data is available.

LP Storage Co-Dispatch

All storage types (battery 4hr/8hr, LDES 100hr, green H₂ 1000hr) co-optimized simultaneously via linear programming. Rolling windows match storage duration: daily for batteries, weekly for LDES, monthly for H₂.

Inter-Regional Flows

Hourly net import/export profiles from EIA-930 data reduce each ISO's self-sufficiency requirement. NEISO counts HQ hydro imports, CAISO imports from PNW. Toggle on/off on the Setup page.

Demand Response

Price-elastic load curtailment with ORDC-linked dynamic triggers. PJM has 10 GW registered DR at $100+/MWh, ERCOT 5 GW at $200+. Fully vectorized activation. Set Off/Low/Med/High on Setup page.

IPM Trigger Indicators

6 automated flags detect when screening approximations are unreliable: VRE cannibalization, tight RA margin, high congestion, storage dominance, retirement cascade, nuclear at risk. Medium/High severity with recommended production model.

Tech-Differentiated Queue Caps

Per-technology interconnection limits (solar, wind, offshore, nuclear, CCS) based on LBNL “Queued Up 2024” completion rates. Solar deploys faster than nuclear. 20% flex pool across technologies.

Data Tier Warnings

Color-coded indicators on the Setup page show data quality per ISO: green (physics data), amber (defaults), red (synthetic). Results page shows a warning banner when running on synthetic data.

Confidence Visualization

Trajectory results show confidence zones: Calibrated (2025–2030), Moderate Extrapolation (2030–2040), High Uncertainty (2040–2060). Background bands and KPI badges communicate model reliability.

Trajectory Backtesting

Backward-looking validation framework runs the model from 2020 conditions with actual historical fuel prices and compares predicted vs observed outcomes (clean %, LMP, emissions) across all 7 ISOs.

Plant-Level Retirement

Individual plant economics drive retirement decisions. Each generator is evaluated by its specific heat rate, revenue, and margin. A 15% per-zone reserve margin reliability floor prevents over-retirement. Nuclear plants assessed individually by contract economics.

Result Provenance

Every simulation result includes audit metadata: git commit SHA, config hash, timestamp, Python version, and a full input snapshot. Enables exact reproducibility and traceability for governance and compliance.

Correlated Scenarios

5 IEA-aligned scenario bundles (STEPS, APS, NZE, High Friction, Rapid Transition) pair input dimensions that co-move in reality. Complements the independent 1,215-scenario sweep with internally consistent narratives.

LMP Extrapolation Guard

Confidence factor degrades as VRE penetration exceeds the model’s calibration range (>60%). IPM trigger warns when LMP pricing is beyond observed data, recommending production-model validation.

Sensitivity Analysis

Morris method screening identifies which input parameters drive the most output variance. Tornado diagrams and variance decomposition help prioritize which assumptions to refine.

Cross-Validation

Structured comparison against EIA AEO, NREL ReEDS, and EPA IPM reference trajectories. Divergences classified as “expected” (mechanism difference) or “unexplained” (potential calibration issue).

Note: This tool trades dispatch fidelity for parametric coverage — 1,215+ scenarios in minutes rather than one scenario in days. It identifies which conclusions are robust across uncertain futures. When results cross into territory requiring dispatch precision (flagged by IPM triggers), validate specific findings with production-grade tools (IPM, GenX, PLEXOS).

How to Use It

  1. Choose a simulation mode Snapshot runs a single point-in-time simulation (fast, ~5-15 seconds). Trajectory projects forward through multiple years with learning curves. Sweep runs 270 scenarios across all parameter combinations.
  2. Select ISO region(s) Pick one ISO for Snapshot mode, or multiple ISOs for Trajectory/Sweep. Each ISO has different demand profiles, fossil fleet composition, and capacity market structures.
  3. Set market parameters Adjust fuel prices, carbon price, clean energy LCOEs, federal incentives, storage costs, and more. Each input has an ⓘ info icon — hover over it to see what the parameter controls and typical ranges. Use L/M/H preset buttons for quick selection, or enter any custom value.
  4. Configure advanced options Set Inter-Regional Imports (On/Off) to include or exclude cross-border power flows. Set Demand Response (Off/Low/Med/High) to model price-elastic load curtailment. These appear at the bottom of the Setup page.
  5. Choose scenario mode Select Independent Sweep for a full 1,215-scenario parameter sweep, or Correlated Bundles to run IEA-aligned scenario packages (STEPS, APS, NZE, High Friction, Rapid Transition) where input dimensions co-move realistically. Correlated bundles are ideal for narrative-driven analysis; independent sweeps are better for mapping the full sensitivity space.
  6. Click "Run Market Simulation" The model will compute results (typically 5-30 seconds for Snapshot/Trajectory). You'll be redirected to the results page automatically.
  7. Review results The results page shows KPI summary cards, LMP profiles, supply stack, merit-order dispatch, generator economics, and deployment profitability. Each chart has an info hover explaining what it shows. Check the lmp_confidence_factor badge on LMP charts — values below 1.0 indicate the model is extrapolating beyond its calibrated VRE range and results should be validated with a production dispatch model.
  8. Check provenance metadata Every result JSON includes a provenance block with the git commit SHA, config hash, timestamp, and full input snapshot. Use this for audit trails and to reproduce any past simulation exactly.
  9. Export & compare Each simulation run is automatically saved to the results/ directory with a unique ID. You'll find CSV data files, chart images, a narrative summary, and an HTML snapshot of the results page.

Interpreting Results

Top-Level KPI Cards

Market Clean %

The percentage of generation from clean sources after the model computes economic dispatch. Higher = more fossil retirement.

Avg LMP ($/MWh)

Time-weighted average locational marginal price. Driven by the marginal fossil generator's cost — as clean penetration rises, this typically falls.

Annual Emissions (MT CO2)

Total annual CO2 emissions from the fossil fleet. Drops as clean generation displaces coal and gas.

Nuclear Revenue ($/MWh)

Total revenue stack for existing nuclear: energy + capacity + 45U PTC. If this falls below the retirement threshold, nuclear plants shut down.

Result Fields Reference

The results JSON includes several specialized fields. Here's how to interpret them:

plant_retirements

Array of individual plant retirement decisions per simulation year. Each entry includes plant_id, plant_name, capacity_mw, fuel_type, heat_rate, annual_profit, and retirement_reason (e.g., "negative_margin", "reserve_margin_floor"). Plants with positive margins stay online; unprofitable plants retire unless doing so would breach the 15% zonal reserve margin floor.

lmp_confidence_factor

A value between 0.0 and 1.0 indicating how far the simulation's VRE penetration is from the model's calibration range. 1.0 = fully within calibrated range (≤60% VRE). 0.5–0.9 = moderate extrapolation (60–80% VRE) — directionally useful but validate key findings. <0.5 = high extrapolation (>80% VRE) — LMP results should be cross-checked with a production dispatch model (IPM, PLEXOS).

tornado_data

Morris method sensitivity output. Contains parameter (input name), mu_star (mean absolute elementary effect — overall influence), sigma (standard deviation — interaction/nonlinearity), and output_metric (which KPI is affected). Parameters with high mu_star are the ones worth refining first. High sigma relative to mu_star indicates the parameter interacts strongly with other inputs.

provenance

Audit metadata block embedded in every result. Fields: git_sha (exact code version), config_hash (SHA-256 of all input parameters), timestamp (ISO 8601), python_version, and input_snapshot (complete copy of all inputs). To reproduce a result, check out the git_sha and run with the input_snapshot. The config_hash lets you quickly verify if two runs used identical inputs.

Charts & Visualizations

LMP & Capacity Revenue

Hourly price profile (Snapshot) or year-over-year average LMP trajectory. Shows how energy prices evolve as the generation mix changes.

Supply Stack

Generation mix by fuel source (TWh). Shows the shift from fossil to clean resources under your assumptions.

Merit-Order Stack

Fossil generators ordered by marginal cost ($/MWh). Generators above the LMP line are uneconomic and face retirement pressure.

Generator Economics Table

Per-unit economics: capacity, dispatch hours, capacity factor, revenue, cost, and profit margin. Color-coded status (profitable / marginal / retiring).

Generator Profitability

Revenue vs. cost per generator class (Trajectory mode). Shows which resources are profitable to deploy at each projection year.

CCS Retrofit vs. New Gas

Carbon price crossover analysis. Shows the carbon price at which CCS-CCGT becomes cheaper than unabated gas.

Zonal LMP Spread

When zonal decomposition is active (requires fleet data), shows per-zone average LMP and price spread vs system average. Identifies congestion-constrained pockets with elevated prices.

Demand Response Metrics

When DR is enabled (Low/Med/High), shows total curtailed GWh, peak activation GW, hours of DR activation, and average price during DR events. Appears as KPI cards below the main results.

VRE Capture Rates

Solar and wind capture rates show the ratio of generation-weighted LMP to average LMP. A capture rate below 1.0 means the resource earns less per MWh than the market average — this is the cannibalization effect. Solar capture rates typically fall to 0.6–0.8 at high penetration due to the duck curve.

IPM Trigger Cards

Colored alert cards appear when results cross reliability thresholds. Red = High severity (production modeling recommended). Amber = Medium severity (results approximate). Each card names the specific limitation and recommends a production model (IPM, PLEXOS, or GenX) to validate the finding.

Confidence Zones

Trajectory charts show colored background bands: green (2025–2030, calibrated), yellow (2030–2040, moderate extrapolation), red (2040–2060, high uncertainty). KPI cards show a confidence badge for the selected year.

Data Tier Warning Banner

When running on synthetic data (Minimal tier), a prominent banner warns that results are illustrative only. Green header = full pipeline data. Amber = partial. Red = synthetic. Check the Setup page data tier indicators for details.

Custom Data Inputs

You can override the model's built-in price curves with your own time-series data. Custom input files go in the custom-user-inputs/ folder inside the market-simulator directory.

How to Use Custom Files

  1. Open the template CSV Find the template in custom-user-inputs/. Each file is pre-filled with the model's default values.
  2. Replace values with your data Keep the header row and column structure. Fill in your price data. Leave ISOs you don't need as-is.
  3. Save as the same filename Rename the template by removing template_ prefix (e.g., lmp_hourly.csv). Save in the same folder.
  4. Enable on the Setup page On the simulation setup page, check the "Use custom file" toggle for the relevant input category. The tool will show whether the file was found and validated.

Important: Custom files must match the exact column headers (ISO names) and row counts shown in the templates. Files with missing columns, wrong row counts, or NaN values will be rejected and the model will fall back to defaults.

Fleet Configuration & Climate Scenario Analysis

The tool includes the full Constellation Energy fleet of 146 plants (15 nuclear, 43 renewable, 81 fossil, 7 storage) sourced from the canonical CEG_fleet_rosetta.csv asset roster. Constellation has no coal assets. Configure fleet decarbonization scenarios directly from the Fleet Climate Scenarios page using the built-in sidebar, or pre-configure plants on the Fleet Config page.

  1. Open Fleet Climate Scenarios Go to Fleet Climate Scenarios to see P10/P50/P90 emissions envelopes for 4 pre-built scenarios: Baseline, CCS Top Emitters, Retire Peakers + CCS Baseload, and Full Transition. Use the checkbox target selectors to overlay one or both climate targets (SBTi 1.5C Power Sector v2 and Absolute Target) simultaneously.
  2. Browse the fleet by category Plants are grouped into four categories: Nuclear (~190 TWh equity-weighted), Renewables (geothermal ~9 TWh, wind/solar/hydro ~4.8 TWh combined), Fossil (81 gas and oil plants), and Storage (7 battery facilities). Each category section is expandable/collapsible.
  3. Configure plant statuses Non-fossil plants (nuclear, renewables, storage) show Operating / Retired toggle only. Fossil plants additionally offer CCS Retrofit — but only for CCS-eligible plants. Non-eligible fossil plants show Operating / Retired only.
  4. Recalculate in real-time Click "Recalculate" to run fleet dispatch against scenario results entirely in your browser. Non-fossil plants use static capacity factors (nuclear 92%, geothermal 85%, wind 30%, solar 22%, hydro 40%). All four charts (fan chart, waterfall, generation mix, emissions by fuel) update instantly with your custom configuration.
  5. Save and compare Name your configuration and click "Save Scenario" to add it as a new scenario in the comparison view. Toggle it on/off alongside the pre-built scenarios.
