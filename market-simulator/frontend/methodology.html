Methodology Disclosure

Model Methodology

An honest assessment of what this tool does, what it doesn't do, and when you should (and shouldn't) use it.

1. What This Tool Does

This is a reduced-form market screening model that evaluates generator profitability, clean energy deployment economics, and retirement pressure across 7 US ISOs under parametric sensitivity scenarios.

Core Capabilities

  • Merit-order dispatch — Stacks fossil generators by marginal cost, dispatches in order until demand is met. Clean resources dispatch at zero marginal cost.
  • ORDC scarcity pricing — Operating Reserve Demand Curve models scarcity structurally: price = MC + VOLL × LOLP(reserves) where LOLP is a logistic sigmoid of operating reserves vs. target. ISO-calibrated VOLL ($2K–$5K/MWh) and reserve targets.
  • VRE cannibalization feedback — Solar and wind revenue uses time-matched LMP (capture rates), not average LMP. Capture rates decline with VRE penetration (duck curve effect). ORDC scarcity hours create a revenue floor preventing total collapse.
  • Zonal LMP decomposition — 2–5 zone pipe-and-bubble model per ISO with ORDC integration. LP solver with dual prices as zonal marginals.
  • LP storage co-dispatch — Simultaneous optimization of battery 4hr/8hr, LDES 100hr, and green H₂ via scipy linprog. Rolling windows match storage duration.
  • Multi-stream revenue decomposition — Energy revenue (LMP × capacity factor) + capacity market payments + REC/PTC/ITC incentives, per resource type.
  • Profit-driven deployment — Deploys clean resources where expected revenue exceeds LCOE. Clean energy level is an output of market economics, not a mandated target.
  • Wright's Law learning curves — Technology-specific cost reduction per doubling of cumulative deployment, with FOAK/NOAK bounds calibrated to published literature.
  • Demand response — Price-elastic curtailment with ORDC-linked dynamic triggers. Fully vectorized (numpy boolean masks). Off/Low/Medium/High levels.
  • IPM trigger framework — 6 automated indicators flag when screening approximations are unreliable, recommending production-model validation.
  • Nuclear retirement risk — Revenue floor model evaluating energy + capacity + §45U PTC against operating cost thresholds.
  • CCS retrofit breakeven — Carbon price breakeven analysis for CCGT retrofits under 45Q credit scenarios.
  • Plant-level retirement — Individual plant economics drive retirement (G1). Each generator evaluated by plant-specific heat rate, fuel cost, and multi-stream revenue. 15% per-zone reserve margin reliability floor. Nuclear plants assessed individually by contract economics, not retired as a fleet.
  • Economic new-build fossil — Endogenous construction of new gas CCGT, gas CT, and coal capacity when market economics support it. Triggered by resource adequacy gaps (reserve margin < 15%) or positive net margins above minimum capacity factor thresholds. Per-ISO CAPEX tables with L/M/H cost levels; coal blocked in CAISO/NYISO/NEISO. Creates a self-correcting feedback loop with ORDC scarcity pricing: low reserves → scarcity spikes → new fossil viable → reserves restored → scarcity subsides. User-configurable min CF, CAPEX overrides, and on/off toggle.
  • Fleet vs grid fossil reconciliation — Two independent new-fossil mechanisms (grid-level market-driven builds and fleet-level user-defined additions) follow an additive interaction rule. Both are tracked and reported separately with overlap detection when both trigger in the same region and year.
  • Tech-differentiated queue caps — Per-technology interconnection limits from LBNL “Queued Up 2024” completion rates. 20% flex pool across technologies.
  • Data tier warnings — Color-coded indicators distinguish synthetic vs. EIA-930 vs. pipeline vs. full-fidelity data quality.
  • Client-side fleet dispatch — The fleet_dispatch.py logic (efficiency ratios, CCS ramp schedules, economic retirement, year-aware retirement/addition masks) has been ported to JavaScript for real-time browser-based recalculation. Users can modify fleet configurations and see updated P10/P50/P90 emissions envelopes across all 1,215 scenarios without a backend round-trip.
  • Interactive web interface — Real-time parametric sensitivity with immediate visual feedback, designed for stakeholder accessibility.
  • Result provenance — Every simulation output includes audit metadata: git SHA, config hash, timestamp, and full input snapshot for reproducibility and governance (G3).
  • Correlated scenario bundles — 5 IEA-aligned scenarios (STEPS, APS, NZE, High Friction, Rapid Transition) with internally consistent input pairings, complementing the independent sweep (G4).
  • LMP extrapolation guard — Confidence factor degrades as VRE penetration exceeds calibration range (>60%). IPM trigger flags results for production-model validation (G5).
  • Cross-validation framework — Structured comparison against EIA AEO, NREL ReEDS, and EPA IPM reference trajectories with expected vs. unexplained divergence classification (G6).
  • Morris sensitivity analysis — Formal sensitivity decomposition identifies which inputs drive the most output variance. Tornado diagrams and variance decomposition (G7).

2. What This Tool Does NOT Do

This tool is not a production capacity expansion model. It complements — rather than replaces — tools like GenX, ReEDS, PLEXOS, IPM, and Aurora for detailed planning analysis.

  • No co-optimized generation + storage + transmission — Storage is co-dispatched via LP within a given resource mix, but the resource mix itself is not endogenously co-optimized with storage (unlike GenX/ReEDS).
  • No unit commitment with physical constraints — UC constraints (min up/down times, ramp rates, start-up costs) are applied post-hoc, not co-optimized with dispatch.
  • Simplified zonal transmission — Pipe-and-bubble zonal model (2–5 zones/ISO) with aggregate transfer limits captures 60–80% of congestion effects, but is not physics-based power flow.
  • Reduced-form VRE cannibalization — Capture-rate feedback is included (solar/wind revenue uses time-matched LMP), but curtailment is not explicitly modeled — the sigmoid depression is an approximation, not a physics-based curtailment dispatch.
  • No probabilistic reliability — No LOLE/EUE calculations. Resource adequacy enforced as a 15% reserve margin floor. ORDC scarcity pricing provides a structural price signal but not a reliability metric.
  • No cross-sector coupling — Electricity only; does not model transport, heat, or industrial electrification interactions.

3. When to Use This Tool

✓ Recommended Uses

  • Directional sensitivity analysis — "Does higher gas price accelerate clean deployment?" The model gets direction right even if magnitude is approximate.
  • Nuclear retirement risk screening — "At what gas + capacity market + PTC combination does nuclear become uneconomic?" Revenue stacking works well here.
  • CCS retrofit breakeven — Carbon price analysis for CCGT retrofits; doesn't depend on dispatch model accuracy.
  • Stakeholder education — Interactive UI helps non-technical audiences understand which variables drive electricity markets.
  • Scenario ranking (relative, not absolute) — "Scenario A produces 15% more clean energy than B" is more reliable than the absolute percentage in either.
  • Identifying ISOs/scenarios for detailed modeling — Triage which combinations warrant a full IPM/PLEXOS run.

✗ Not Recommended

  • Absolute LMP forecasting — Values include ORDC scarcity pricing and zonal decomposition, but lack nodal transmission and proper unit commitment.
  • Optimal resource portfolio design — Capture-rate feedback and LP storage are included, but the tool does not co-optimize generation mix + storage + transmission endogenously.
  • Policy impact quantification — "A $50/ton carbon price reduces emissions by X MT" — direction right, magnitude unreliable.
  • Plant-specific retirement timing — Heuristic retirement model cannot predict when individual plants close.
  • Investment decisions or regulatory filings — Results are screening-quality, not investment-grade.

6. Known Limitations

Ranked by impact on results. Critical limitations affect results materially; moderate limitations affect interpretation.

Critical Limitations

  1. Reduced-form VRE cannibalization — Capture-rate feedback is included (solar/wind revenue uses time-matched LMP), but curtailment is modeled via a sigmoid depression approximation, not explicit hourly curtailment dispatch. ORDC scarcity hours create a revenue floor. Results are reasonable to ~60% VRE penetration; above that, IPM trigger cards recommend production dispatch validation.
  2. Simplified zonal transmission — Pipe-and-bubble zonal model (2–5 zones/ISO) with ORDC-integrated scarcity pricing captures 60–80% of congestion effects. Not physics-based power flow — misses nodal-level price separation, loop flows, and contingency constraints.
  3. Post-hoc unit commitment — UC constraints applied after dispatch, not co-optimized. Misses minimum up/down times, ramp rates, and start-up costs, leading to incorrect LMP during shoulder hours.
  4. No endogenous capacity expansion — Storage is LP-co-dispatched within a given mix, but the generation + storage + transmission mix is not jointly optimized (unlike GenX/ReEDS). Resource deployment is profit-driven sequential, not globally optimal.
  5. Synthetic data fallback — When pipeline parquets are absent, the model uses synthetic profiles that are not calibrated to physics. Results are illustrative only. The Setup page shows color-coded data tier indicators, and the Results page shows a warning banner when synthetic data is active.

Moderate Limitations

  1. Static supply model — Doesn't account for price-induced supply responses (e.g., high EAC prices stimulating additional investment).
  2. Reduced-form demand response — Price-elastic curtailment with ORDC-linked dynamic triggers, not full DR resource dispatch. Fully vectorized but uses a linear ramp activation model. Captures first-order demand elasticity and scarcity interaction but not DR-as-a-resource economics.
  3. Simplified inter-regional trade — Import/export from EIA-930 historical data; not price-responsive.
  4. Policy snapshot (early 2025) — RPS, IRA credits, and GHG Protocol evolve; model frozen at 2025 assumptions.
  5. Plant-level retirement — Individual plant economics now drive retirement (G1 improvement); however, real closure decisions also depend on factors the model does not capture (decommissioning costs, state intervention, long-term contracts). Fleet-fraction fallback used when plant-level data is unavailable.
  6. Trajectory confidence degrades — Beyond 2030: moderate extrapolation. Beyond 2040: high uncertainty. Assumptions about cost curves, demand growth, and policy become speculative. Confidence zones and backtesting validation communicate this (see Section 7).

Automated Limitation Detection

The model includes 6 IPM trigger indicators that automatically flag when results cross thresholds where these limitations become binding — e.g., VRE penetration above 40% triggers a cannibalization warning, tight reserve margins flag the need for UC-constrained modeling. These serve as a triage mechanism, identifying where investment in a production-grade model run (IPM, PLEXOS, GenX) would materially change results.

The tool is positioned as a complement to production models, not a replacement. Use it to narrow the parameter space and identify which scenarios warrant full IPM/GenX analysis.
