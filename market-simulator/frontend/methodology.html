Methodology Disclosure

Model Methodology

An honest assessment of what this tool does, what it doesn't do, and when you should (and shouldn't) use it.

1. What This Tool Does

This is a fossil dispatch overlay on exogenous clean generation shapes. The upstream hourly-cfe-optimizer pipeline generates physics-feasible clean energy mixes and optimizes their costs. This tool takes those clean generation profiles as inputs, subtracts them from demand to derive residual fossil load, and dispatches the fossil fleet against that residual — computing LMP formation, plant-level economics, retirement trajectories, emissions, and capacity adequacy across 7 US ISOs.

The core question is not "what does clean energy cost?" — that's answered upstream. The core question is: "Given this clean generation backdrop, what happens to the incumbent fossil fleet?"

Core Capabilities

  • Merit-order dispatch — Stacks fossil generators by marginal cost, dispatches in order until demand is met. Clean resources dispatch at zero marginal cost.
  • ORDC scarcity pricing — Operating Reserve Demand Curve models scarcity structurally: price = MC + VOLL × LOLP(reserves) where LOLP is a logistic sigmoid of operating reserves vs. target. ISO-calibrated VOLL ($2K–$5K/MWh) and reserve targets.
  • VRE cannibalization feedback — Solar and wind revenue uses time-matched LMP (capture rates), not average LMP. Capture rates decline with VRE penetration (duck curve effect). ORDC scarcity hours create a revenue floor preventing total collapse.
  • Zonal LMP decomposition — 2–5 zone pipe-and-bubble model per ISO with ORDC integration. LP solver with dual prices as zonal marginals.
  • LP storage co-dispatch — Simultaneous optimization of battery 4hr/8hr, LDES 100hr, and green H₂ via scipy linprog. Rolling windows match storage duration.
  • Multi-stream revenue decomposition — Energy revenue (LMP × capacity factor) + capacity market payments + REC/PTC/ITC incentives, per resource type.
  • Unified merit-order deployment — Clean and fossil resources compete head-to-head in a single ranked list sorted by net cost. Resources deploy cheapest-first regardless of type. Clean energy level is an output of market economics, not a mandated target. A $41/MWh gas CCGT will deploy before a $95/MWh nuclear plant — the model is economics-neutral.
  • Wright's Law learning curves — Technology-specific cost reduction per doubling of cumulative deployment, with FOAK/NOAK bounds calibrated to published literature.
  • Demand response — Price-elastic curtailment with ORDC-linked dynamic triggers. Fully vectorized (numpy boolean masks). Off/Low/Medium/High levels.
  • IPM trigger framework — 6 automated indicators flag when screening approximations are unreliable, recommending production-model validation.
  • Nuclear retirement risk — Revenue floor model evaluating energy + capacity + §45U PTC against operating cost thresholds.
  • CCS retrofit breakeven — Carbon price breakeven analysis for CCGT retrofits under 45Q credit scenarios.
  • Plant-level retirement — Individual plant economics drive retirement (G1). Each generator evaluated by plant-specific heat rate, fuel cost, and multi-stream revenue. 15% per-zone reserve margin reliability floor. Nuclear plants assessed individually by contract economics, not retired as a fleet.
  • Economic new-build fossil — Endogenous construction of new gas CCGT, gas CT, and coal capacity when market economics support it. Triggered by resource adequacy gaps (reserve margin < 15%) or positive net margins above minimum capacity factor thresholds. Per-ISO CAPEX tables with L/M/H cost levels; coal blocked in CAISO/NYISO/NEISO. Creates a self-correcting feedback loop with ORDC scarcity pricing: low reserves → scarcity spikes → new fossil viable → reserves restored → scarcity subsides. User-configurable min CF, CAPEX overrides, and on/off toggle.
  • Fleet vs grid fossil reconciliation — Two independent new-fossil mechanisms (grid-level market-driven builds and fleet-level user-defined additions) follow an additive interaction rule. Both are tracked and reported separately with overlap detection when both trigger in the same region and year.
  • Tech-differentiated queue caps — Per-technology interconnection limits from LBNL “Queued Up 2024” completion rates. 20% flex pool across technologies.
  • Data tier warnings — Color-coded indicators distinguish synthetic vs. EIA-930 vs. pipeline vs. full-fidelity data quality.
  • Full-fleet Rosetta-based dispatch — The 146-plant Constellation Energy fleet (15 nuclear, 43 renewable, 81 fossil, 7 storage) sourced from CEG_fleet_rosetta.csv. Non-fossil plants dispatched via static capacity factors (nuclear 92%, geothermal 85%, wind 30%, solar 22%, hydro 40%). Fossil plants use the merit-order dispatch engine. CCS retrofit restricted to CCS-eligible plants only. Four fleet scenarios: baseline, CCS top emitters, retire peakers + CCS baseload, full transition.
  • Client-side fleet dispatch — The fleet dispatch logic (efficiency ratios, CCS ramp schedules, economic retirement, year-aware retirement/addition masks) runs in JavaScript for real-time browser-based recalculation. Users can modify fleet configurations and see updated P10/P50/P90 emissions envelopes without a backend round-trip. Backend serves Rosetta-derived sample data as primary source, sweep-based results as fallback.
  • Interactive web interface — Real-time parametric sensitivity with immediate visual feedback, designed for stakeholder accessibility.
  • Result provenance — Every simulation output includes audit metadata: git SHA, config hash, timestamp, and full input snapshot for reproducibility and governance (G3).
  • Correlated scenario bundles — 5 IEA-aligned scenarios (STEPS, APS, NZE, High Friction, Rapid Transition) with internally consistent input pairings, complementing the independent sweep (G4).
  • LMP extrapolation guard — Confidence factor degrades as VRE penetration exceeds calibration range (>60%). IPM trigger flags results for production-model validation (G5).
  • Cross-validation framework — Structured comparison against EIA AEO, NREL ReEDS, and EPA IPM reference trajectories with expected vs. unexplained divergence classification (G6).
  • Morris sensitivity analysis — Formal sensitivity decomposition identifies which inputs drive the most output variance. Tornado diagrams and variance decomposition (G7).

1b. Methodological Approach — Parametric Robustness vs. Single-Point Optimization

Traditional capacity expansion models (IPM, GenX, PLEXOS) solve a constrained optimization: "What's the least-cost generation portfolio?" They deliver a single "optimal" plan per scenario. Running 5–10 scenarios takes days to weeks.

This tool takes a fundamentally different approach. The 1,215-scenario parametric sweep evaluates fossil fleet consequences across thousands of plausible futures, delivering P10/P50/P90 distributions rather than single point estimates. This trades dispatch fidelity for parametric coverage — showing which findings hold across a wide range of input assumptions rather than what's optimal for one assumed future.

Single-Point Optimization (IPM/GenX) Parametric Sweep (This Tool)
Core question "What's optimal for this assumed future?" "Which findings hold across a wide range of assumptions?"
Assumption treatment Fixed inputs → one "optimal" solution Swept inputs → probability-weighted distribution
Fragility Small input changes can flip outcomes ($2/MWh gas shift changes retirement timing by years) P10/P50/P90 bands explicitly show where outcomes are assumption-sensitive
Decision support Prescriptive: "Build 43.2 GW solar" Descriptive: "Solar + battery is no-regrets above 70% clean across all fuel/carbon scenarios"
Runtime Hours to days per scenario 5 sec – 30 min (full 1,215-scenario sweep)
Scenario coverage 3–10 reference cases 1,215+ parametric combinations

For corporate procurement, technology investment timing, and policy screening — all decisions under uncertainty — knowing which findings survive across a range of assumptions is often more actionable than a single optimal answer contingent on getting every assumption right. The decision-relevant question is "which investments hold up even if we're wrong about gas prices?" not "what's the optimal portfolio if gas is $4.50/MMBtu?"

Context in Energy Systems Analysis

Multi-scenario analysis with distributional output is increasingly common in energy planning, though this tool's approach is simpler than formal uncertainty frameworks:

  • NREL Standard Scenarios — NREL publishes 30+ scenarios annually, explicitly stating that no single scenario should guide investment. The parametric sweep here follows the same logic — more scenario coverage reveals which findings are assumption-dependent — though NREL runs a full capacity expansion model (ReEDS) for each scenario, which this tool does not.
  • IEA World Energy Outlook — Shifted from a single "reference case" to three named scenarios (STEPS, APS, NZE), an acknowledgment that single-point forecasts are insufficient. This tool extends that principle to 1,215 combinations but with a simpler dispatch model.
  • EPRI US-REGEN — Runs 100+ scenarios across technology cost, policy, and demand assumptions, presenting results as ranges. Similar philosophy, much higher dispatch fidelity.

The tool does not implement formal robust optimization (Bertsimas, Ben-Tal), stochastic programming, or DMDU/RDM frameworks. It's a brute-force parametric sweep — run the same dispatch model 1,215 times with different inputs and report the distribution. The value comes from coverage and speed, not methodological sophistication.

Where single-point optimization retains its advantage: Regulatory proceedings (FERC/PUCs require specific plans), transmission planning (specific lines in specific locations), and short-term operations (exact commitment schedules). The IPM trigger system (§9b) bridges this gap: when parametric results cross into territory requiring dispatch fidelity, the model flags it automatically.

2. Retirement Mechanism — Plant-Level Economics

Fossil generator retirement is driven by per-plant economic evaluation, not fleet-fraction heuristics. Each generator is individually assessed based on its specific heat rate, fuel cost, capacity factor, and multi-stream revenue (energy + capacity + RECs).

Per-Plant Margin Calculation

For each plant i in the fossil fleet, the model computes an annual operating margin:

margini = revenueenergy(CFi × LMP) + revenuecapacity + revenueancillary − costfuel(HRi × Pfuel) − VOMi − FOMi

Where HRi is the plant-specific heat rate (Btu/kWh), CFi is capacity factor from merit-order dispatch, and Pfuel is the regional fuel price. Plants with margin < −$5/MWh are retired, worst-first.

Reliability Floor Constraint

A 15% per-zone reserve margin floor prevents over-retirement. Even if a plant is uneconomic, it is retained if its retirement would push zonal reserves below the reliability threshold. This mirrors the reliability-must-run (RMR) contracts used by ISOs in practice.

Nuclear Per-Plant Evaluation

Nuclear plants are evaluated individually by contract economics, not retired as a fleet. Each unit's offtake contract revenue is compared against operating costs. Plants with below-market offtake contracts face higher stranding risk than those with regulated-rate or market-rate contracts. The §45U Production Tax Credit is included in the revenue stack where applicable.

Full mathematical formulations for the retirement engine, including the merit-order sorting algorithm, reliability floor enforcement, and inter-year persistence of retired plant IDs, are documented in Model Methodology Specification §2.

3. LMP Formation & Confidence

Hourly Locational Marginal Prices (LMPs) are computed from a merit-order fossil dispatch stack with ORDC scarcity pricing and zonal decomposition. Several safeguards ensure appropriate confidence communication.

Confidence Degradation at High VRE Penetration

The demand-quantile pricing layer is empirically calibrated to 2019–2024 ISO State of Market data. As VRE penetration increases beyond the calibration range, a continuous sigmoid confidence factor smoothly degrades LMP reliability. This eliminates artificial jumps at fixed thresholds:

VRE PenetrationConfidence FactorInterpretation
≤ 50%~1.0Fully within calibration range
60%~0.97Near-calibration boundary
70%~0.87Moderate extrapolation — directionally reliable
78% (midpoint)~0.70Transition zone — magnitudes increasingly uncertain
85%~0.55Significant extrapolation — use with caution
90%~0.48Beyond calibration — results are indicative only
95–100%~0.40Floor — minimum confidence

Calibration Range Disclosure

LMP formation coefficients (demand-quantile shape, ORDC parameters, scarcity pricing sigmoid) are calibrated against observed 2019–2024 wholesale market data from ISO State of Market reports. Results within this calibration window are grounded in historical actuals; results beyond it are extrapolated with decreasing confidence.

IPM Trigger Integration

When VRE penetration exceeds 60%, the model automatically raises an IPM trigger indicator recommending production-model validation. At >75%, the trigger severity escalates to "critical." These triggers are surfaced in the Results page as colored alert cards with specific recommendations for which production model (IPM, PLEXOS, GenX) would address the identified limitation.

4. Scenario Construction

The model uses two complementary approaches to scenario analysis: an independent parametric sweep covering 1,215+ combinations, and 5 correlated scenario bundles with internally consistent input pairings.

Correlated Scenario Bundles

Five IEA World Energy Outlook-aligned scenarios provide coherent narratives where input parameters move together as they would in the real world:

Scenario Gas Price Renewable LCOE Carbon Price Demand Growth Narrative
STEPS Medium Medium Low Medium Current policy trajectory
APS Medium Low Medium Medium Announced pledges implemented
NZE High Low High Low Net Zero by 2050
High Friction High High Low High Supply chain constraints, slow transition
Rapid Transition Low Low High Medium Accelerated clean energy adoption

Correlated bundles complement the independent sweep: the sweep explores the full parameter space (including implausible corners), while the bundles highlight the most policy-relevant trajectories with internally consistent assumptions.

Sensitivity Analysis — Morris Method & Variance Decomposition

Formal sensitivity decomposition identifies which input parameters drive the most output variance. The model implements two approaches:

  • Morris method (elementary effects) — Screens 9 input dimensions to rank parameters by influence on key outputs (clean share, total cost, LMP, retirement pace). Produces tornado diagrams showing first-order sensitivity rankings.
  • Variance decomposition — Decomposes total output variance into contributions from each input dimension and interaction effects. The 1,215-scenario sweep results contain all the data needed; this is a post-processing analysis applied to cached results.

Results show which assumptions matter most for each ISO, enabling decision-makers to focus analytical effort on the parameters with the highest leverage.

4b. Fleet Dispatch — Rosetta-Based Methodology

The fleet scenario analysis operates on the full Constellation Energy fleet of 146 plants (15 nuclear, 43 renewable, 81 fossil, 7 storage) derived from the canonical CEG_fleet_rosetta.csv data source. Constellation has no coal assets.

Data Provenance

The fleet data pipeline flows through two build scripts:

  • CEG_fleet_rosetta.csv → generate_constellation_scenarios.py → constellation_scenarios.json (plant-level configuration with CCS eligibility, heat rates, CO&sub2; rates, capacity factors)
  • CEG_fleet_rosetta.csv → build_fleet_scenario_data.py → fleet_scenario_results_sample.json (pre-computed scenario results)

Nuclear generation uses equity-weighted capacity, totaling ~190 TWh. Geothermal contributes ~9 TWh (Geysers complex). Wind/solar/hydro contribute ~4.8 TWh combined.

CCS Retrofit is restricted to CCS-eligible fossil plants only. CCS eligibility is determined by plant characteristics in the Rosetta CSV (fuel type, heat rate, capacity, siting feasibility). Non-fossil plants (nuclear, renewables, storage) render in the sidebar grouped by category with Operating/Retired toggle only — no CCS option is shown.

5. What This Tool Does NOT Do

This tool is not a production capacity expansion model. It deliberately trades dispatch fidelity for parametric coverage — evaluating fossil fleet consequences across 1,215+ scenarios in minutes rather than one scenario in days. These limitations are the cost of that tradeoff. The IPM trigger system (§9b) automatically identifies when dispatch fidelity matters and production-grade tools (GenX, PLEXOS, IPM, Aurora) should validate a specific finding.

  • No co-optimized generation + storage + transmission — Storage is co-dispatched via LP within a given resource mix, but the resource mix itself is not endogenously co-optimized with storage (unlike GenX/ReEDS).
  • No unit commitment with physical constraints — UC constraints (min up/down times, ramp rates, start-up costs) are applied post-hoc, not co-optimized with dispatch.
  • Simplified zonal transmission — Pipe-and-bubble zonal model (2–5 zones/ISO) with aggregate transfer limits captures 60–80% of congestion effects, but is not physics-based power flow.
  • Reduced-form VRE cannibalization — Capture-rate feedback is included (solar/wind revenue uses time-matched LMP), but curtailment is not explicitly modeled — the sigmoid depression is an approximation, not a physics-based curtailment dispatch.
  • No probabilistic reliability — No LOLE/EUE calculations. Resource adequacy enforced as a 15% reserve margin floor. ORDC scarcity pricing provides a structural price signal but not a reliability metric.
  • No cross-sector coupling — Electricity only; does not model transport, heat, or industrial electrification interactions.

6. When to Use This Tool

✓ Recommended Uses

  • Directional sensitivity analysis — "Does higher gas price accelerate clean deployment?" The model gets direction right even if magnitude is approximate.
  • Nuclear retirement risk screening — "At what gas + capacity market + PTC combination does nuclear become uneconomic?" Revenue stacking works well here.
  • CCS retrofit breakeven — Carbon price analysis for CCGT retrofits; doesn't depend on dispatch model accuracy.
  • Stakeholder education — Interactive UI helps non-technical audiences understand which variables drive electricity markets.
  • Scenario ranking (relative, not absolute) — "Scenario A produces 15% more clean energy than B" is more reliable than the absolute percentage in either.
  • Identifying ISOs/scenarios for detailed modeling — Triage which combinations warrant a full IPM/PLEXOS run.

✗ Not Recommended

  • Absolute LMP forecasting — Values include ORDC scarcity pricing and zonal decomposition, but lack nodal transmission and proper unit commitment.
  • Optimal resource portfolio design — Capture-rate feedback and LP storage are included, but the tool does not co-optimize generation mix + storage + transmission endogenously.
  • Policy impact quantification — "A $50/ton carbon price reduces emissions by X MT" — direction right, magnitude unreliable.
  • Plant-specific retirement timing — Heuristic retirement model cannot predict when individual plants close.
  • Investment decisions or regulatory filings — Results are screening-quality, not investment-grade.

9. Known Limitations

Ranked by impact on results. Critical limitations affect results materially; moderate limitations affect interpretation.

Addressed Gaps (G1–G7)

The following limitations from the original peer review have been substantively addressed:

GapIssueResolutionStatus

G2 Missing methodology equations LP zonal decomposition and VRE cannibalization equations added to Model Methodology Specification Implemented
G3 No result provenance Git SHA, config hash, timestamp, and full input snapshot embedded in every simulation output Implemented

Remaining Limitations (G8–G11)

  1. G8: Code quality improvements — Type hint coverage ~5%, no CI/CD test pipeline. Does not affect model results; affects maintainability and contributor onboarding.
  2. G9: No WACC / financing cost toggle — LCOE calculations include implicit WACC but no user-configurable sensitivity for interest rates, project risk premiums, or PPA structures.
  3. G10: Demand response parameters frozen — DR parameters calibrated to 2023–2024 values. FERC Order 2222 implementation could materially change DR availability by 2028–2030.
  4. G11: Single weather year — Default uses 2024 weather year. 2021–2025 sensitivity available but not integrated into the parametric sweep. Weather-year variability in VRE output is not captured in uncertainty bands.

Critical Limitations (Structural)

These are inherent to the model's reduced-form design and are not planned for resolution:

  1. Reduced-form VRE cannibalization — Capture-rate feedback is included (solar/wind revenue uses time-matched LMP), but curtailment is modeled via a sigmoid depression approximation, not explicit hourly curtailment dispatch. ORDC scarcity hours create a revenue floor. Confidence degrades above 60% VRE (see Section 3).
  2. Simplified zonal transmission — Pipe-and-bubble zonal model (2–5 zones/ISO) with ORDC-integrated scarcity pricing captures 60–80% of congestion effects. Not physics-based power flow — misses nodal-level price separation, loop flows, and contingency constraints.
  3. Post-hoc unit commitment — UC constraints applied after dispatch, not co-optimized. Misses minimum up/down times, ramp rates, and start-up costs, leading to incorrect LMP during shoulder hours.
  4. No endogenous capacity expansion — Storage is LP-co-dispatched within a given mix, but the generation + storage + transmission mix is not jointly optimized (unlike GenX/ReEDS). Resource deployment uses a unified merit order (clean and fossil ranked together by net cost), not a global LP.
  5. Synthetic data fallback — When pipeline parquets are absent, the model uses synthetic profiles that are not calibrated to physics. Results are illustrative only. The Setup page shows color-coded data tier indicators, and the Results page shows a warning banner when synthetic data is active.

Automated Limitation Detection

The model includes 6 IPM trigger indicators that automatically flag when results cross thresholds where these limitations become binding — e.g., VRE penetration above 60% triggers LMP extrapolation warnings with confidence degradation, tight reserve margins flag the need for UC-constrained modeling. These serve as a triage mechanism, identifying where investment in a production-grade model run (IPM, PLEXOS, GenX) would materially change results.

The tool is positioned as a complement to production models, not a replacement. Use it to narrow the parameter space and identify which scenarios warrant full IPM/GenX analysis.

ISO-Level Divergence Summary

ISO-level comparisons use a 30 pp threshold (vs. 20 pp for national) to account for the structural offset of comparing regional results against national reference trajectories.

  • CAISO: +8 to +36 pp vs. references — driven by SB100 mandate (100% clean by 2045). Only CAISO 2040 vs. AEO (+36 pp) exceeds the investigation threshold.
  • ERCOT: −1 to +12 pp — close alignment with ReEDS; slightly above AEO due to strong wind economics in a no-mandate ISO.
  • PJM: −13 to −0.1 pp — below ReEDS (weaker mandates than national cost-optimal path); near-perfect agreement with AEO Reference.

Divergence is informative, not diagnostic. These models answer different questions: our simulator models profit-maximizing generators; ReEDS minimizes system cost; AEO projects current policy. Large divergence doesn't mean either model is wrong — it means structural assumptions produce different outcomes. Full results with per-ISO, per-year breakdowns and investigation notes are available in Cross-Validation Results.
