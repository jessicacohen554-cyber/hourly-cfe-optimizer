## 3. Does the VRE-as-standalone framing hold up?

**Yes, with an important nuance the research makes explicit.**

The research draws a sharp distinction between *generic standalone VRE* (deteriorating) and *targeted standalone VRE within a firm-clean portfolio* (highly attractive). Key findings:

- **VRE is NOT viable as a pure merchant play.** The research is unequivocal: "the business case for generic standalone VRE in the late 2020s is rapidly deteriorating." Negative pricing, cannibalization, and basis risk make unhedged VRE unbankable in CAISO and increasingly risky in ERCOT.

- **VRE IS viable as a standalone pipeline decision within the portfolio context.** The research validates the exact framing: VRE as a "REC engine" during daylight hours, with firm clean covering nights and storage capturing arbitrage. The three assets are financially unified but physically standalone.

- **The "standalone VRE sweet spot" exists:** PJM solar (capture ~95% today, high-value localized RECs, capacity market payments) and ERCOT solar+storage (fast interconnection, arbitrage revenue, massive DC demand pipeline). CAISO standalone solar is explicitly a "Sell."

- **Storage pairing is economically optimal but not existentially required in PJM.** PJM solar capture rates remain high enough (~95% today, ~70% by 2030) that standalone solar without storage still generates positive returns. Storage adds +169% revenue uplift but the floor without it is above zero in PJM. In ERCOT, storage pairing is effectively mandatory.

**Bottom line:** The standalone framing holds because the VRE pipeline is a discrete capital allocation decision. The IPP isn't asking "should we build VRE instead of firm clean?" — it's asking "does adding VRE to our existing portfolio create incremental value?" The research answers yes, decisively, in PJM and ERCOT.

## 4. Single most important new insight

**The GHG Protocol Scope 2 update will disqualify 30-50% of existing corporate REC portfolios, forcing a massive wave of localized procurement that directly benefits IPPs with VRE in constrained deliverability regions.**

This is the strongest new data point because it transforms the demand picture. The current deck frames demand as "hyperscalers need more clean energy." The research reframes it as "hyperscalers need to *replace* 30-50% of their existing clean energy claims with locally-sourced, hourly-matched alternatives." This means the addressable market isn't just the incremental gap from new DC load — it includes the forced repricing and resourcing of existing portfolios. The REC price multiplier (3-7x) under deliverability constraints makes PJM VRE assets dramatically more valuable than the deck currently suggests.
