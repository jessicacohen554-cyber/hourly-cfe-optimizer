LMP Cannibalization: Why Standalone VRE Underperforms

5

The Three-Headed Challenge

Price Cannibalization

Zero-marginal-cost generation drives wholesale prices toward $0 during peak production. CAISO solar captures <30% of average LMP; ERCOT wind at 54%.

Basis Risk

Nodal price divergence from congestion means power generated in cheap zones can’t reach load. Hyperscalers now discount PPAs for basis exposure.

ELCC Collapse

PJM assigns solar ELCC of ~8%. A 100 MW solar farm gets paid for 8 MW of capacity. Standalone VRE capacity value is effectively zero.

Bottom line: A merchant standalone solar asset modeling standard forward curves without adjusting for capture-rate degradation will fail its DSCR and equity IRR hurdles. This is precisely why VRE must be paired with storage and sold as a shaped product within a diversified clean portfolio.

The 24/7 CFE Premium: Why Hyperscalers Will Pay More

9

From Annual Matching to Hourly CFE

The shift from annual volumetric matching to 24/7 hourly CFE is creating a structural pricing premium that fundamentally changes VRE economics.

Annual matching: Buy RECs to offset total consumption. Allows fossil reliance at night. Low cost, low impact.
24/7 hourly CFE: Match consumption with clean generation every hour. Requires storage to cover night/low-wind. Higher cost, real emissions reduction.

Validated by Landmark Deals

  • Microsoft + Constellation: 835 MW Three Mile Island nuclear restart — 20-year firm PPA at premium pricing
  • Google + Broadwing: 400 MW gas + CCS — paying for both power AND carbon capture
  • Google + AES: 24/7 CFE portfolios using solar + wind + storage arrays
  • Meta + Constellation: 20-year Clinton nuclear PPA for firm, 24/7 clean baseload
  • Amazon + Talen: 1.92 GW Susquehanna nuclear — largest single-asset nuclear PPA
Product Availability Paradox (Eurelectric/EY): Extreme complexity of structuring 24/7 CFE products creates supply scarcity → higher margins for developers who can deliver.

Investment Thesis Summary

Demand is structural, not cyclical. AI inference scales linearly with adoption. The 231 GW US DC pipeline is committed capital with 3–5 year build horizons.
The green premium is structural and expanding. Hyperscalers are executing multi-GW deals at $35–50/MWh premiums above market. GHG Protocol Scope 2 updates (est. 2027) will disqualify 30–50% of existing corporate REC portfolios, forcing localized hourly procurement — regional REC premiums expected to increase 3–7×.
Storage is the unlock. Pairing 4–8hr BESS with VRE transforms the revenue stack (+169%), captures capacity value, and meets the 24/7 CFE product that buyers demand.
The window is finite — and the vertical integration signal is real. Procurement Accounting: What It Means for Where to Buy

14

Three competing procurement strategies are fragmenting the market — but the VRE+Storage investment thesis holds under all of them. Strategy choice shapes where to weight the portfolio, not whether to invest. Note: GHG Protocol Scope 2 consultation is moving toward hourly matching + deliverability (Citation #35), which structurally favors Strategy 2 and increases total VRE demand.

Three Strategies, One Thesis

① Consequential (Cross-Regional)
Buy cheapest $/tCO₂ clean energy anywhere in the US. No hourly constraint. Buyer picks lowest-cost ISO. Drives new-build in MISO/SPP/ERCOT where wind is cheapest.
Buyers: Meta, Amazon (large MISO/SPP unbundled REC purchases). Incentive: Cheapest path to high CFE % — VRE pipeline in wind-rich ISOs is the product.
② Hourly Matching (Same-ISO)
Match consumption with clean generation every hour, same ISO. Forces storage + firm pairing. Requires 1.7× over-procurement at 95% CFE to cover nights and low-wind.
Buyers: Google (24/7 CFE), Microsoft (nuclear PPAs). Incentive: VRE+Storage is the only product that satisfies hourly matching — mandatory pairing = higher $/MWh but guaranteed offtake.
③ Annual Volumetric (Status Quo)
Match total MWh over the year. Cheapest unbundled RECs satisfy targets — high paper CFE %, low emissions impact. Being phased out by leading buyers.
Incentive: Minimal VRE pipeline value — existing RECs suffice. Sunset strategy as SBTi/SEC tighten standards.

The MISO Question: How Much Committed Load Makes It Work?

Meta + Amazon = the demand signal. Both companies use consequential accounting. MISO VRE pipeline value depends on whether their committed DC load actually materializes and drives procurement:
  • 5% C&I participation (hyperscaler-only): 17 TWh clean demand → ~7 GW new VRE at $49/MWh — still buyable from existing pool
  • 10% participation (+ corporate followers): 34 TWh → ~13 GW VRE. Still $49/MWh. Pipeline value emerging but not dominant.
  • 20–30% (broad adoption): 69–103 TWh → existing pool exhausts. New-build wind + solar required. This is the inflection point where MISO VRE pipeline becomes a must-own.
Strategy-proof portfolio (PJM, ERCOT, NYISO — 80% weight): VRE+Storage holds value regardless of which accounting method dominates.
Strategy-leveraged hedge (MISO, SPP — 15–20%): Upside if consequential persists + Meta/Amazon load materializes. Downside protected by cheap basis (pivot to storage-paired if hourly wins).
Bottom line: The market will adopt a hybrid of strategies — Google hourly, Meta consequential, most corporates annual. Fragmentation increases total VRE demand.
